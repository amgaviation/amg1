## 0. Why this document exists

AMG sells coordination. The owner contracts with AMG, the owner pays the pilot directly, and AMG's invoice carries one line: the coordination fee. AMG never furnishes the aircraft, never takes operational control, and never makes a go/no-go decision.

That structure protects AMG from the illegal-charter problem. It does not protect AMG from the other problem — the one that actually ends companies:

**An airplane gets bent, and the insurer finds the pilot was never approved to fly it.**

The hull claim gets denied. The owner's insurer looks for someone to subrogate against. The only party in the chain who touched the pilot's qualifications and is not the owner is AMG. If AMG's file is thin, AMG is the defendant. If AMG's file is complete — and shows the underwriter approved that pilot in writing before the flight — AMG is a witness.

That is the entire difference. This checklist is the difference.

**Language discipline.** AMG does not "verify the pilot." That claim creates a duty of care AMG is not insured to carry. What AMG says, in writing, in marketing, on the phone, and in the agreement:

> We confirm certificates, currency and time-in-type, and we put the pilot's qualifications in front of your broker for written approval before the flight.

That sentence is accurate, it is defensible, and it describes exactly what the gate below does.

## 1. The gate

Run in this order. Do not reorder. Items 1 through 5 are AMG collecting and confirming. Item 6 is the owner's insurer approving. Item 7 applies only to permit ferries.

**No item may be skipped, deferred to after departure, or satisfied verbally.**

---

### GATE 1 — CERTIFICATE LEVEL. COMMERCIAL OR ATP. NEVER A PRIVATE PILOT.

> ### ⛔ A PRIVATE PILOT MAY NOT ACT AS PIC FOR COMPENSATION OR HIRE.
> ### 14 CFR 61.113(a). There is no exception AMG can source a pilot into.
> ### If the certificate says PRIVATE, the job stops here. Every time. No callback, no workaround, no "the owner is fine with it."

**Who provides:** the pilot — a legible copy or photo of both sides of the airman certificate.
**Done looks like:** an image in the trip file showing COMMERCIAL PILOT or AIRLINE TRANSPORT PILOT, the certificate number, and the ratings line. Not a typed number in an email. Not a verbal read-out. The document.

**The failure mode — read this one twice.**

This does not break on the contract pilot AMG sources. Contract pilots hold Commercial or ATP; that is their job. It breaks on the person the owner brings.

The setup is always the same. The owner has a friend — 4,000 hours, thirty years flying, owned three airplanes, knows the make and model better than anyone. The friend holds a **private** certificate. The owner's insurance requires a second pilot or a mentor pilot for the first N hours in type. The owner says: "Tony, I've got my guy for the right seat, I just need you to coordinate it and handle the paperwork." Or the owner asks AMG to put the friend on the job and pay him a day rate.

The friend's total time is irrelevant. The owner's comfort is irrelevant. The moment that pilot is compensated — day rate, expenses beyond a pro-rata share, a fuel card, anything of value — and acts as PIC, it is a 61.113(a) violation. And AMG, having placed him, is in it.

**Watch for the disguises:**

- "He's not being paid, the owner's just covering his hotel and rental car." Compensation includes goods and services. Air-carrier and enforcement precedent has treated logged flight time itself as compensation. Do not litigate the edge — the certificate level is the bright line, and AMG stays on the safe side of it.
- "He's only the second pilot, he's not PIC." Then Gate 5 applies, and the underwriter question in Section 5 has to be answered before anything moves. A private certificate still cannot be compensated for the right seat on a Part 91 flight where the second pilot is a required crewmember or is being paid as one.
- "He's going to give the owner some instruction on the way." Instruction requires a CFI (14 CFR 61.193). See Section 5 — this is the setup that voids the insurance requirement the owner was trying to satisfy.
- "It's a ferry, it's not really a trip." 61.113(a) does not care what kind of flight it is.

**The script when the certificate comes back PRIVATE:**

> "I can't put him on this. A private certificate can't act as PIC for compensation under 61.113(a), and your underwriter is going to look at exactly that after a claim. I can source a Commercial or ATP pilot with time in your make and model and get him approved in writing by your broker. Your friend is welcome in the airplane — he just can't be the paid pilot on it."

Log the refusal in the trip file. A documented "no" is an asset.

### GATE 2 — MEDICAL, RATINGS, TYPE RATING, LAST RECURRENT

**Who provides:** the pilot.
**Collect:**
- Current medical certificate — class, issue date, date of birth (drives duration). Confirm it is current for the privileges being exercised on this specific flight.
- Ratings: category, class, instrument. Confirm they match the aircraft and the planned operation.
- **Type rating** where the aircraft requires one, with any limitations shown (SIC privileges only, VFR only, circling limitations, etc.).
- **Last recurrent training completion certificate** — the actual graduation certificate from the training provider, showing the aircraft, the course, and the completion date.

**Done looks like:** documents in the file. A recurrent completion certificate is the single item underwriters ask for most and the one pilots most often "will send later." It does not go later. It goes before.

---

### GATE 3 — TIMES

**Who provides:** the pilot, on AMG's pilot data sheet, signed and dated.
**Collect:** total time, multi-engine, turbine, make-and-model (this specific type), and last-90-day times. Instrument time and time in type in the last 12 months if the underwriter's form asks.

**Done looks like:** a signed, dated sheet. The pilot attests to his own numbers — AMG transmits them, AMG does not audit a logbook and does not represent the numbers as verified. If a number looks implausible against the certificate and recurrent record, ask once, in writing, and keep the answer.

---

### GATE 4 — DOMESTIC PART 91 SIC: 61.55(b) CURRENCY

Applies whenever the pilot is serving as **second in command** in an aircraft type certificated for more than one required pilot, or in an operation requiring an SIC.

**Requirement:** 14 CFR 61.55(b) — within the preceding **12 calendar months**, in that type, the pilot has completed the required familiarization and flight training items, appropriately logged and endorsed.

**This is not an SIC type rating.** 61.55(a)(3) exempts operations conducted domestically within the United States. Pilots and owners confuse these constantly, and so do some brokers. AMG's job is to know the difference and to collect the right thing: the 61.55(b) logbook page or endorsement, dated inside 12 calendar months, for that type.

**Done looks like:** the logged/endorsed 61.55(b) entry in the file, with the date and the type visible.

---

### GATE 5 — W-9 AND THE PILOT'S OWN NON-OWNED COI

**Who provides:** the pilot.
**Collect:**
- Signed IRS Form W-9. The pilot is an independent contractor engaged by the owner. AMG does not pay the pilot and does not issue his 1099 — but AMG collects the W-9 so the owner can, and so AMG can show the relationship was structured correctly.
- **Certificate of insurance for the pilot's own non-owned aircraft liability policy**, in force through the trip dates, with limits shown.

**Done looks like:** both documents in the file, COI dates bracketing the job.

---

### GATE 6 — WRITTEN EMAIL FROM THE OWNER'S BROKER OR UNDERWRITER

> ### NO EMAIL, NO FLIGHT.

**Who provides:** the aircraft owner's insurance broker or underwriter — not the owner, not the shop, not the pilot.

**What it must say:** it must name **that specific pilot** and confirm he is either (a) within the open pilot warranty on that policy for that aircraft, or (b) added by endorsement. It must be in writing. It must come from the broker's or carrier's own email address.

**Not acceptable:**
- The owner saying "my broker says we're fine."
- A forwarded policy declarations page with an open-pilot clause and no pilot named.
- A verbal from the broker, however senior.
- A text message from anyone.
- Anything that arrives after wheels-up.

**Turbine assumption:** for any turbine aircraft, assume there is **no usable open pilot warranty** and that a written endorsement is required. Budget **24 to 72 hours**. This is not pessimism — it is the planning number. See Section 3.

**How this gets sold, not apologized for.** The approval step is the product. The pitch:

> "The step that takes the time is getting your underwriter to name the pilot in writing. That's also the step that keeps your hull coverage intact if anything goes sideways. I'm not going to move your airplane without it, and you don't want me to."

Never promise "someone there tomorrow morning" on a jet. On a piston with a genuine, confirmed-in-writing open pilot warranty, next-morning is sometimes real. On a turbine, quote 24-72 hours for approval from the moment the complete pilot package goes to the broker.

### GATE 7 — PERMIT FERRIES: THE BROKER ACKNOWLEDGES THE FERRY

Applies only when the aircraft is moving under a **special flight permit** (out of annual, expired inspection, or an unairworthy discrepancy) under 14 CFR 21.197.

**Requirement:** the broker's or underwriter's **written acknowledgment of the ferry** — in the same email that names the pilot, or in a clearly linked reply on the same thread. Hull and liability coverage frequently contains an airworthiness condition. A ferry under a special flight permit is, by definition, a flight of an aircraft that does not currently meet the standard airworthiness requirements. If the carrier has not acknowledged that in writing, the coverage question is open, and AMG will have coordinated a flight into an open coverage question.

**AMG's role in the permit itself: none.**

- **AMG must never be the applicant or the signer.** Not on the application, not on any attachment, not "as agent for" the owner.
- The **owner, or the owner's A&P/IA**, submits **FAA Form 8130-6** (the application) under 14 CFR 21.199.
- The **FSDO or a DAR** issues **FAA Form 8130-7** (the certificate) with **operating limitations attached**.
- 21.197 itself contains no compensation language. The restrictions that bind this flight come from the **operating limitations issued with the permit under 21.199**, enforceable through **14 CFR 91.9(a)**. Read them. They routinely restrict route, altitude, day/VFR, persons on board, and required equipment.
- **21.197(c) continuing authorizations are not available to a Part 91 owner.** Every AMG-coordinated ferry needs its own individual permit. There is no standing permit to lean on.

**Done looks like:** a copy of the issued **8130-7** *and* its attached **operating limitations** in the trip file, both read by the coordinating pilot, **before launch**. Not the application. The issued certificate.

## 3. When the answer is "the open pilot warranty covers it"

This will happen. The owner will say it, the broker will say it casually on the phone, and the pilot will say it because he wants the work. It is sometimes true. It is never sufficient by itself.

### Why a verbal OPW answer is not enough

An open pilot warranty is a set of **minimums** written into the policy — typically some combination of certificate level, total time, multi-engine time, retractable-gear or turbine time, make-and-model time, instrument rating, and a recurrent training requirement within the preceding 12 months. Whether a given pilot meets all of them is a factual question about numbers that only the pilot has, checked against policy language only the carrier controls.

"He's covered" is somebody's opinion until the carrier writes it down. After a hull loss, the opinion is worth nothing and the carrier's reading of its own warranty is worth everything.

### How to get it in writing anyway, without friction

Do not argue with the broker. Do not ask him to reconsider. Ask him to type the sentence he just said.

**Phone script:**

> "That's what I was hoping. Do me a favor — put that in an email to me and copy [owner]. Something like: 'Confirming [pilot name] meets the open pilot warranty on [N-number] as currently written.' That's it, one line. It's for the owner's file and mine. I don't fly anybody without it."

**Email version — reply on the existing thread:**

```
Thanks [Broker first name]. So I have it in the file, can you reply to this
with the following, or your own wording to the same effect:

  "Confirming that [pilot full name], Commercial/ATP cert. no. [number],
  meets the open pilot warranty on [Owner]'s policy for [N-number] as
  currently written, for the operation described. No endorsement required."

One line is all I need. Copying [Owner] so it's in his records too.
```

Brokers do this readily. It costs them thirty seconds and it protects them too — the broker who told you verbally and got it wrong is also exposed. Frame it that way if you meet resistance.

### Why a turbine almost never has usable OPW

Assume, as a planning default, that **there is no usable open pilot warranty on a turbine aircraft.**

Reasons this holds up in practice:

1. **Turbine policies often name pilots outright.** Instead of a warranty with numeric minimums, the policy lists approved pilots by name. Anyone not on the list requires an endorsement. Full stop.
2. **Where a warranty exists, the make-and-model minimum is the killer.** A warranty requiring, say, meaningful time in type plus initial or recurrent training in that specific type from an approved provider excludes exactly the pilot AMG is trying to place — a competent, current, typed pilot who is new to *this* owner's airplane.
3. **The recurrent requirement is specific.** Not "recurrent training" generally — recurrent in that make and model, from a named or approved provider, within 12 months. Trip-file Gate 2 exists to answer this before the broker asks.
4. **Ferry and repositioning flights sit outside routine use.** Even a pilot who clears the warranty may trigger a separate look when the flight is a permit ferry.

**So the planning rule is:**

| Aircraft | Assume | Quote the owner |
|---|---|---|
| Piston, pilot clearly clears the numbers | OPW may apply — still get the one-line email | Same day to next morning, subject to broker confirmation |
| Piston, pilot marginal on any minimum | Endorsement | 24-48 hours |
| Turboprop / light jet, any pilot | **Written endorsement required** | **24-72 hours from complete package to broker** |
| Any permit ferry | Endorsement + ferry acknowledgment | 24-72 hours, and the 8130-7 has its own timeline |

When an owner pushes on the timeline, the answer is not an apology:

> "I can have a qualified pilot committed today. What I can't compress is your underwriter naming him in writing, and that's the piece that keeps your hull claim from being denied. On a jet that's typically one to three business days. I'll send the package to your broker within the hour and push it every day until it's back."

### Retention

| Category | Keep for | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Full trip file | **7 years minimum** after the trip date | Covers Florida's statute of limitation windows for contract and negligence claims with margin, and covers the interval in which a late-reported claim or subrogation demand realistically arrives |
| Insurance approval emails, waiver of subrogation, coordination agreement | **10 years**, or permanently | These are the documents that decide whether AMG is a defendant. Storage is free. Reconstruction is impossible |
| Permit ferry file (8130-7 + limitations) | **10 years** | FAA enforcement and civil exposure both run long on airworthiness questions |
| Tax records — invoices, W-9s | 7 years | Standard |
| Declined-request log | Permanently | Cumulative evidence of a compliance practice, and worth more each year it grows |

**Never deleted early to save space. Never deleted at an owner's request.**

## 5. Mentor and second-pilot jobs

The highest-margin work and the highest-risk work. Read this before quoting one.

### The situation

An owner buys a step-up airplane. The insurer conditions coverage on some number of hours with a second pilot, or a mentor pilot, in the right seat before the owner flies it solo. The owner needs a body who satisfies the requirement. AMG coordinates that body.

Three things go wrong here, and they compound.

### 5.1 The certificate — Gate 1 breaks here more than anywhere

Repeat: **the owner's high-time private-pilot friend cannot be the paid second pilot.** 61.113(a). Total time, type familiarity, and thirty years of friendship do not create an exception. This is the single most common way the gate gets tested, because the owner has already decided and is asking AMG to process a decision rather than make one.

Say no early, say it once, and offer the replacement in the same breath.

### 5.2 The currency — 61.55(b), not an SIC type rating

If the pilot is acting as **SIC** in a type certificated for more than one required pilot:

- Required: **14 CFR 61.55(b)** — familiarization and flight training in that type, completed and logged/endorsed within the preceding **12 calendar months**.
- **Not** required for domestic US operations: an **SIC type rating**. 61.55(a)(3) exempts operations conducted within the United States.

Owners, pilots, and occasionally brokers ask for "the SIC type rating." Answer plainly:

> "For a domestic Part 91 flight he doesn't need an SIC type rating — 61.55(a)(3) exempts domestic operations. What he does need is 61.55(b) familiarization and flight training in type inside the last 12 calendar months, logged and endorsed. I have his endorsement and it's attached."

Getting this right in front of a broker is a credibility moment. Getting it wrong costs the relationship.

### 5.3 The question that has to be asked in writing: PILOT or INSTRUCTOR?

Before sourcing anyone for a mentor or second-pilot job, put this to the owner's underwriter **in writing** and keep the answer in `03-Insurance`:

```
Subject: Coverage requirement clarification — [N-NUMBER] — second pilot or
instructor?

[Broker first name],

Before I source anyone for [Owner], I need the requirement stated precisely
so I put the right person in the right seat.

The policy condition for [Owner]'s first [x] hours in [make/model] requires:

  (a) a qualified SECOND PILOT / SIC — a Commercial or ATP pilot meeting
      stated minimums, acting as a crewmember, with the owner as PIC and
      sole logger of PIC time; or

  (b) a FLIGHT INSTRUCTOR — a CFI holding the appropriate category, class
      and instrument authorization for this aircraft, giving and logging
      dual instruction that the owner logs as instruction received?

These are different people with different certificates and I don't want to
satisfy one requirement with the other. If it is (b), please also confirm
```

### 5.4 The illegal setup — name it out loud

**The common arrangement AMG will not coordinate:**

A "mentor pilot" who is **not a CFI** sits in the right seat, talks the owner through the airplane, and the owner logs the time as **dual instruction received**.

Why it fails, in order:

1. **Only a CFI may give flight instruction.** 14 CFR 61.193 sets out what a flight instructor is authorized to train and endorse; a pilot without an instructor certificate is not authorized to give it. There is no instruction being legally given, so there is no instruction to receive.
2. **The owner's logbook is then wrong.** Time logged as dual received from someone who cannot give it is a false entry. That is its own problem the day anyone examines the logbook — which is exactly the day after an accident.
3. **And here is the part that matters commercially: it voids the requirement it was meant to satisfy.** The owner accepted a policy condition requiring instruction. He satisfied it with a person who cannot legally provide instruction. The condition was never met. The insurer, reviewing the file after a loss, sees an unmet policy condition — and the owner's coverage is exposed on precisely the flight hours he bought the mentor to protect.
4. **If that mentor is also a private pilot being paid**, add 61.113(a) on top. Now there are two independent grounds and the file is unsalvageable.

**AMG's position, verbatim, to the owner:**

> "If your underwriter is requiring instruction, I need to put a CFI in that seat, and he logs it as instruction given. If they're requiring a second pilot, I need a Commercial or ATP who meets their minimums, and he flies as SIC — you log PIC, he logs SIC. What I can't do is put a non-instructor in the right seat and have you log dual. That doesn't satisfy the condition your policy is asking for, and it puts your coverage on those exact hours in question. Let me get the requirement in writing from your broker and I'll source the right person."

### 5.5 Sourcing rules for mentor work

- CFI requirement confirmed → source a **CFI** with the appropriate category/class/instrument authorization, type rating where required, and demonstrated time in make and model. Same Gate 1-6 run. The CFI's certificate copy shows the instructor certificate **and** the expiration date — check it.
- Second pilot requirement confirmed → source a **Commercial or ATP** pilot, run 61.55(b) at Gate 4, and confirm in writing with the owner who is logging what.
- Either way, the **written underwriter approval naming that individual** (Gate 6) is still required. A confirmed requirement is not a confirmed pilot.

## Standing rules

1. **No email, no flight.** There is no owner important enough, no maintenance slot valuable enough, and no fee large enough to launch a pilot who is not named in writing by the owner's underwriter.
2. **Private certificate is a full stop**, not a negotiation.
3. **AMG never signs the 8130-6** and never appears as applicant on a special flight permit.
4. **AMG does not "verify" anyone.** AMG confirms certificates, currency and time-in-type, and puts the pilot's qualifications in front of the broker for written approval before the flight.
5. **The waiver of subrogation is pre-launch**, not post-invoice.
6. **Every "no" gets written down.** The declined-request log is the cheapest insurance AMG owns.

# 08 — The First 90 Days

AMG Aviation Group. North Lauderdale, FL. Owner: Antonio Gonzalez.
Sequencing document. Everything else in the launch kit is a tool; this is the order you pick the tools up.

Read this once end to end, then work off the checklists. The numbers in here are the real numbers, not the encouraging ones.

---

## What AMG is selling, restated once so the plan stays honest

AMG coordinates people, not airplanes. Four services: contract pilot sourcing, maintenance ferry and repositioning coordination, second/mentor/insurance-required pilot coordination, and short-term flight department overflow.

The owner contracts with AMG. The owner pays the pilot directly. AMG's invoice has one line — the coordination fee. No markup, no rebates, no trip funds through AMG's account. AMG never furnishes the aircraft.

Every revenue idea in the next 90 days has to survive that structure. Several obvious growth moves do not, and they are named below rather than left for later.

## Week 0 — Become transactable

Week 0 is not a week of selling. It is the shortest possible run-up to being able to take money and put a pilot on an airplane. It should take five to seven days of part-time work, not thirty.

### Blocks selling — do not talk to a stranger about money without these

| Item | Why it blocks | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| Entity formed, EIN issued, business bank account open | You cannot invoice or be paid as a business | Bank login works, EIN letter saved |
| Stripe account in **live mode**, one successful real charge | You need to collect the fee same-day | $1 charge to your own card, refunded, visible in the dashboard |
| A signable Coordination Services Agreement (doc 01) — your version, pre-counsel | You cannot start work on a handshake | PDF exists, e-sign link tested on your phone |
| Waiver of subrogation clause present in that agreement | Single most valuable line in the document; do not sell a job you cannot paper | Clause is in the draft you send, not in a "to be added" list |
| Insurance gate checklist (doc 02) printed and in your flight bag | It is the operational product | You can run it from memory |

Stripe note: Stripe only ever sees the coordination fee. Trip funds, pilot pay, hotels, fuel — none of it moves through AMG. If a customer tries to send you pilot pay, refuse it and re-explain the structure. That refusal is a selling moment, not an obstacle.

### Blocks the first flight, not the first conversation

- **AMG's own aviation liability / E&O quotes.** This is the long pole. Start it Day 1 of Week 0 and expect two to four weeks.
  - Describe the four services **verbatim** on the application, in AMG's own words, including the sentence that AMG never furnishes aircraft and never takes operational control. Fla. Stat. §627.409 lets an insurer rescind the policy for a material misrepresentation in the application. A policy that gets rescinded at claim time is the same as no policy, purchased at full price.
  - If a broker's application form has no field that fits, attach a one-page service description as a rider and reference it in the application. Keep the sent copy.
  - Get three quotes. Aviation-specialty brokers only; a general commercial agent will mis-class this.
- **Owner-side insurance gate** for the specific job: certificates, currency, time-in-type, 61.55(b) currency for a domestic Part 91 SIC, W-9, the pilot's own non-owned liability COI, and the **written email from the owner's broker or underwriter naming that specific pilot**. No email, no flight. On a special flight permit ferry, the same email must acknowledge the ferry.

### Blocks cold outreach, not warm outreach

- Founder credentials and a real photograph on amgaviationgroup.com. Certificates and ratings you actually hold, stated plainly. No "years of experience" claims you cannot substantiate on paper.
- Real street address published. Not a PO box presented as an office.
- Phone number that you answer, on the site and in the signature block.
- Google Business Profile claimed and verified. Verification is postcard-slow; start it Week 0 so it lands during Days 15-45.

A shop's Director of Maintenance will Google you between the handshake and the callback. A pilot you already know will not.

### Does not block anything — do it in the gaps

Logo refinement. Business cards. LinkedIn page. A second page on the website. Anything with the word "brand" in it.

### The lawyer

Send the agreement to counsel in Week 0. **Do not wait for it to come back before selling.**

Counsel review runs one to three weeks. Fourteen days of not calling anyone is fourteen days of not learning anything, and the redline you get back will be better if you have three real conversations' worth of objections to hand the lawyer alongside it. What counsel must confirm before you take money — not before you make calls — is:

1. Waiver of subrogation in AMG's favor on the owner's hull **and** liability policies.
2. Operational control language: owner and PIC retain all authority; AMG makes no go/no-go, fuel, route, or release decision.
3. Indemnity running the correct direction.
4. That the fee is stated as a coordination fee for a service, never per flight, per leg, or per hour.

If a job appears before the redline is back, sell it on the draft under the four conditions printed at the top of document 01 — including the sentence in the covering email that the agreement is in final legal review. Send that email; do not say it on the phone only. Nobody has ever been offended by that sentence.

### FDACS

Florida Telemarketing Act, §§501.601-501.626. AMG likely needs a commercial telephone seller licence under §501.605 or a §501.604 exemption affidavit. The B2B exemption at §501.604(10) does **not** cover AMG — it requires three years operating under the same name. The exemption that plausibly fits is §501.604(3): no major sales presentation over the phone, sale completed at a later face-to-face meeting.

Call FDACS in Week 0 and confirm in writing which path applies. Meanwhile: manual dial only, no auto-dialer, no bulk SMS, no ringless voicemail, a written DNC policy on file per 47 CFR 64.1200(d), and no call recording — Florida is two-party consent under §934.03.

Structuring the sale as *phone contact, then meet at the shop or the FBO to sign* is good selling and good compliance at the same time. Do it that way regardless of how FDACS answers.

**Week 0 gate:** you can take a card, send a signable agreement, and run the insurance gate from memory. Insurance quotes are out. Then you stop building and start dialing.

## Days 1-14 — The revenue sprint

The purpose of these two weeks is not $10,000. It is to find out whether the offer sells, and to whom.

### Daily targets

- 10 dials, manual, into the warm network — every working day.
- 2 shop visits in person per week, minimum. Walk in with the one-pager (doc 03), not a pitch deck.
- 1 insurance broker or underwriter conversation per week.
- Every conversation logged same day: who, aircraft, what they actually needed, what they said no to.
- Zero days with no outbound contact. Flying days included — you can make three dials in a crew car.

### Channel order, and why

**1. Warm network first (Days 1-5).** Pilots you have flown with, owners you have flown for, chief pilots, the two or three DOMs who already know your name. They do not need the website, the GBP, or the logo. They need to know you are doing this and what it costs. Highest close rate, shortest cycle, and the honest feedback you cannot get from a stranger.

**2. Independent maintenance shops, in person (Days 3-14).** This is the first sale profile: a shop's customer whose aircraft needs to move, with the **owner** as AMG's contracting party. Pitch throughput, not price: an airplane you cannot get onto your ramp is a slot you cannot bill. The shop is a referral source, permanently. It is never the customer, it never pays AMG, and AMG never pays a DOM personally — referral money goes to the **shop entity** against its invoice with a W-9 on file. Fla. Stat. §838.16 makes commercial bribery a third-degree felony, and §838.15 catches the recipient. Employer consent is the line and it needs to be written.

**3. Insurance brokers and underwriters by phone (Days 7-14).** Slowest to convert, most durable. A broker who has an owner with a lapsed-currency problem or an open-pilot-warranty gap has a job for AMG every few months for years. Ask what their approval turnaround actually is on a turbine endorsement, and use the answer in your quoting.

### The arithmetic, both ways

**Fee-only, coordination jobs alone:**

| Coordinated turbine jobs (14 days) | Fee at $895 | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | $895 | $2,685 |
| 6 | $895 | **$5,370** |
| 8 | $895 | $7,160 |
| 11 | $895 | **$9,845** |
| 12 | $895 | $10,740 |

Six jobs in fourteen days is $5,370. The $10,000 end of the goal is **eleven coordinated turbine jobs from a standing start with no customers, no reputation, and no repeat flow**. That will not happen. Say so out loud now so you do not treat a good two weeks as a failure.

**Gross collected, including Tony's own flying — the honest version:**

| Source | Volume | Rate | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tony as contract PIC, light jet trip days | 4 days | $1,800/day | $7,200 |
| Coordination fees, turbine jobs Tony did not fly | 3 events | $895 | $2,685 |
| **14-day gross collected** | | | **$9,885** |

That is reachable. It is also a different business from the one on the invoice, and the difference matters:

**On any trip where Tony is PIC, the coordination fee is dropped entirely.** No $895, no partial fee, no "reduced coordination." That trip runs under a **separate agreement engaging Antonio Gonzalez personally as a contract pilot**, billed as pilot services only. Two documents, two invoices, two roles, and they never appear on the same piece of paper. Charging a coordination fee on a trip you fly is charging a fee for furnishing yourself, and it is the fastest way to make AMG look like something it is not.

One carve-out and no others: if AMG fronts an airline positioning ticket, it is reimbursed as a documented AMG out-of-pocket **at cost**, with the receipt attached. Never as an hourly or per-leg amount. Never inside pilot pay.

Track the two numbers separately from Day 1. **Coordination revenue is the business. Tony's flying is the runway.** If you let them blend into one "revenue" figure you will not notice for six months that the coordination business never actually started.

**Days 1-14 gate:** 50 qualified conversations, at least one signed agreement, at least one completed job of any type. See "The gates" below if you miss.

## Days 15-45 — Repeatability

One job is an anecdote. The question in this window is whether the same customer comes back and whether anyone sends you someone.

### The three targets

1. **First repeat customer.** Same owner, second job. Call them 72 hours after the first job closes — not to sell, to close the loop on how the pilot performed. The second job usually comes out of that call.
2. **First unprompted referral.** A shop, a broker, or a pilot sends someone to you without being asked. When it happens, write down exactly what the referrer said AMG does. That sentence is your marketing copy; it will be better than anything you write.
3. **First prepaid credit block sold.**

### The prepaid credit block

> **Blocked until counsel delivers a block agreement.** Do not sell a block, quote a block, or mention a
> block price to a customer until a written Prepaid Coordination Credit Agreement exists, containing at
> minimum: the refund right below stated verbatim, an express statement that credit is applied only against
> AMG coordination fees for aircraft the buyer owns, the block's precedence over the integration clause in
> §11 of the Coordination Services Agreement, and the balance-on-every-invoice obligation. Add it to the
> counsel engagement in document 01 as a third document and expect the budget to rise accordingly. Selling
> a prepayment against a document that does not exist is how a small company acquires its first genuinely
> bad problem.
>
> Hold unearned block balances in a separate bank account from operating cash. A sentence telling yourself
> not to spend the float is not a control; a second account is.

**Offer:** $5,000 paid up front, drawing down at **$795 per coordinated event** instead of $895. Six events at $795 is $4,770; the $230 remainder stays on the balance or is refunded. Roughly an 11% discount in exchange for cash up front and volume commitment on their side.

**Who can buy it — read this carefully.** The block is bought by an **aircraft owner**: a multi-aircraft owner, a small flight department, or a family office that moves airplanes often enough to justify it.

A maintenance shop **cannot** buy a block covering its customers' aircraft. AMG takes payment from the aircraft owner and nobody else. A shop paying AMG's coordination fee for a job on a customer's airplane makes the shop the customer, breaks the contracting structure, and starts to look like AMG selling movement of aircraft as a package. The only version that works is a shop buying a block for **aircraft the shop itself is the registered owner of** — courtesy aircraft, trade-ins, aircraft it has taken title to.

What you offer a shop instead: a standing referral arrangement, plus the $795 block rate made available to *its customers* who buy their own blocks, with each owner contracting and paying individually. The shop gets the throughput benefit and the goodwill; it never gets an AMG invoice.

**Frame it in exactly these terms, and say all three out loud:**

- It is **prepaid credit against future coordination fees.** It is not a retainer and it is not a subscription. Nothing recurs and nothing auto-renews.
- It is **not guaranteed availability.** AMG does not promise a pilot on any given date. Never say "guaranteed," "24/7," "on call," or "nationwide." What the block buys is a lower per-event fee and first position in the queue when AMG has capacity — priority of effort, not a promise of lift.
- It **does not shorten the insurance gate.** Underwriter approval on a turbine still runs 24-72 hours and AMG cannot compress it. Sell the approval step as the product: the block buys you a coordinator who already has the pilot's file assembled when the owner calls.

**The controller's question — "what happens to our unearned credit if AMG stops trading?"**

Answer it before they ask it, because a good controller will ask and a bad one will just decline. The answer:

> Unearned credit is refundable in cash on 30 days' written notice, for any reason, at any time — you do not have to give a reason and you do not have to have used any of it. On AMG's books it is a liability, not revenue; it is recognized only as each event completes. If AMG stops trading, the unearned balance is refunded within 30 days. That refund right is written into the block agreement, and the balance is stated on every invoice so you always know what it is.

Two things to be straight about internally. First, the refund obligation is an unsecured contractual promise from a small company — AMG is not holding the money in trust, and you should not imply that it is. That is precisely why the block is capped at $5,000 rather than $25,000: keep the exposure small enough that AMG can always make it good out of operating cash. Second, do not spend the float. Treat the unearned balance as somebody else's money in AMG's account, because it is.

Talk to a CPA about revenue recognition before you sell the second block.

**Days 15-45 gate:** one repeat customer, one inbound referral, and either one block sold or a clear read on why not.

## Days 46-90 — Second channel, and the structural decision

Two things happen in this window. You open a second acquisition channel, and you make the decision that determines what AMG is at month twelve.

### Second channel

Pick one, not three. The candidates, in order of expected return:

1. **Insurance brokers as a repeatable pipeline** rather than one-off calls. Build a short list of the aviation brokers writing Part 91 turbine business in Florida and the Southeast. The pitch is that AMG puts a pilot's qualifications in front of them in a clean, complete package for written approval — which makes their file better and their turnaround shorter. Never say AMG "verifies" the pilot; that phrase creates a duty of care AMG is not insured for.
2. **Owner-operator segment direct** — the owner flying a turboprop or light jet who periodically needs a second pilot for insurance-required or mentor work. High recurrence, and the 61.113(a) trap lives here: a mentor or second pilot flying for compensation must hold **commercial or ATP**, never private. This is the failure that breaks most often and it breaks specifically on "mentor" jobs.
3. **Flight departments with one aircraft and two pilots** — the overflow customer. Slow to open, very sticky once open.

### The ceiling problem — face it now, not in month six

$30,000 per month at $895 per event is **33.5 coordinated events per month**. Across roughly 21 business days, that is **1.6 events per business day** — each one sourced, insurance-gated, documented, and shepherded to completion by one person who also flies a line.

It is arithmetically impossible. Not hard. Impossible. The insurance gate alone is 24-72 hours of turnaround per turbine job, plus the phone calls to assemble it, and it does not compress with volume because the underwriter's clock is not yours.

There are exactly three paths out, and they are not mutually exclusive but one of them has to be primary.

**(a) Day-scaled coordination fee.** The flat fee already breaks above two days: $895 is 75-128% of a single turboprop ferry pilot day at published rates, and 3.7% of a 14-day coverage assignment that is far more work. The flat fee is wrong at both ends; day-scaling fixes the top end and only a lower base fixes the bottom. Move to $495/$895 for days 1-2, then **+$150/day piston and +$250/day turboprop & light jet**, with a soft cap at 25-30% of pilot spend. Effect: a 10-day turbine coverage job goes from $895 to roughly $2,900, and 33 events a month becomes about 12. The piston number is a separate open decision, not a day-scaling question: $495 is roughly 100% of one piston pilot day at $400-600, which is heavy for the most price-sensitive segment AMG has. The argument for repricing to $295-$395, and the argument for holding $495 because the coordination work does not vary by aircraft class, are both set out under "Two open pricing decisions" in the kit README. Decide it there and propagate one number. Until then, $495 is the published price and the one to quote.

**(b) Prepaid credit blocks.** One sale produces six events. Sell six blocks and you have 36 events off six closes. This is the only path that reduces *selling* effort rather than *delivery* effort — the coordination work per event is unchanged.

**(c) Tony's own flying as the revenue base, coordination as the margin layer.** Contract flying at $1,500-2,500/day for light jet trip work is the reliable floor. Coordination becomes the higher-margin layer that grows on top of it and eventually replaces the flying days.

**The decision, made here and not deferred:** run **(a) as the primary structural fix, (b) as the volume multiplier, and (c) as the explicit floor for the first twelve months.** Day-scaling goes live no later than Day 60 — it costs nothing but a price sheet revision and it is the single change that most improves revenue per hour of Tony's time. Blocks are the growth mechanism. Tony's flying pays the bills while the first two compound, and it is retired as a revenue line only when coordination clears $12,000/month for three consecutive months.

Write the revised price sheet on Day 46. Do not re-litigate this in month five.

## The gates

Each phase has a condition. Meet it and advance. Miss it and take the stated action — not a new idea.

| Gate | Advance when | If it fails |
|---|---|---|
| **Week 0 → Days 1-14** | Can take payment, send a signable agreement, run the insurance gate from memory. Insurance applications submitted. | Nothing here justifies a delay past Day 7. If you are still building on Day 8, you are hiding. Start dialing with what exists. |
| **Days 1-14 → 15-45** | 50 qualified conversations, ≥1 signed agreement, ≥1 completed job. | See below. |
| **Days 15-45 → 46-90** | ≥1 repeat customer AND ≥1 inbound referral. | No repeat: the delivery experience is the problem, not the offer. Call the last five customers and ask a single question — "what would have made you call me again?" No referral: you are not asking. Ask the next five directly, by name, for one introduction. |
| **Day 90** | Coordination revenue (excluding Tony's flying) ≥ $4,000 in month 3, on ≥5 events, with the day-scaled price sheet live. | Coordination is not a business yet. Keep flying, cut outbound to a fixed 5 dials/day, and stop spending money on it until one channel produces two customers unprompted. |

**If 50 qualified conversations produce zero paid jobs: narrow the offer. Do not add features.**

Zero from fifty is not a coverage problem or a pricing problem. It means the offer is not landing on a specific enough pain. The response is to cut the four services down to the one that got the most heat in those conversations — most likely maintenance ferry coordination for owners at independent shops — and sell only that, to one buyer type, with one sentence. Nothing gets added: not a new service, not a website page, not a brochure, not a "platform." Adding surface area to an offer that is not converting makes it harder to explain and harder to buy.

Re-run 25 conversations on the narrowed offer. If that produces zero, the buyer is wrong, not the offer — change who you are calling, not what you are saying.

## The five failure modes

For each: the observable thing that means it is happening now.

### 1. Insurance denial or rescission

AMG's own policy gets rescinded under Fla. Stat. §627.409 because the application described a business AMG does not run, or the owner's insurer denies a hull claim and subrogates against AMG.

**Early warning:** AMG's application or binder does not contain the four services described in AMG's own words. A signed agreement goes out without a waiver of subrogation in AMG's favor on both hull and liability. A job launches with the broker's approval given verbally or "confirmed by text" instead of in an email naming that specific pilot. A ferry launches without a copy of the 8130-7 **and** its operating limitations in the trip file — remember AMG is never the applicant or signer on the 8130-6, and 21.197(c) continuing authorizations are not available to a Part 91 owner, so every ferry needs its own permit.

**The single tell:** an airplane moved and there is no email in the file with the pilot's name in it. If that has happened once, it will happen again, and the day it matters is the day there is a claim.

### 2. Site claims contradicting the legal position

The website starts describing a business AMG is not allowed to be, and it becomes the exhibit.

**Early warning:** the word "dispatch" appears anywhere. Any sentence implying availability of lift ("we can get you to Teterboro Thursday"). A list of aircraft, anywhere, in any form. "We verify our pilots." "Guaranteed," "24/7," "worldwide," "nationwide," a network size, a mission count, or years of experience you cannot substantiate. A testimonial without written permission from a real customer — FTC 16 CFR Part 465, penalties up to ~$53,088 per violation, and incentivized testimonials must disclose the incentive. An all-in delivered price for moving an airplane. Any price quoted per flight, per leg, or per hour.

**The single tell:** you cannot read a page of the site out loud to an FAA inspector without editing as you go.

### 3. Fourteen days spent on structure instead of on the phone

The most likely failure, by a wide margin, and the most comfortable one.

**Early warning:** it is Day 10 and the dial log has fewer than 60 entries. Any calendar week with a logo, a deck, a CRM, or a website revision in it and fewer than 30 dials. The sentence "I'll start calling once the agreement is back from the lawyer." A fourth service being designed before the first one has a paying customer.

**The single tell:** more hours in the last seven days went into documents than into conversations.

### 4. The month-6 ceiling

Revenue plateaus and the only visible lever is Tony working more hours, which he does not have.

**Early warning:** month 4 and 5 revenue within 10% of each other while hours worked rise. The price sheet is still flat-fee past Day 60. Zero prepaid blocks sold by Day 90. Coordination revenue is being reported blended with Tony's flying so nobody can see that the coordination line is flat. Turning down a job because there was no time to run the gate.

**The single tell:** two consecutive months where coordination events went up and coordination revenue did not.

### 5. Tony's ATP caught in someone else's gray charter

The one that ends the career rather than the company.

**Early warning:** a customer asks AMG to help "find an airplane," or asks for one price to move an airplane door to door. A broker or shop offers to pay AMG's fee on behalf of a passenger. A dry lease shows up where AMG is expected to supply the crew — that is the classic sham dry lease and the answer is no, with no counter-offer. Someone who is not the registered owner or a documented authorized representative signs the agreement. Passengers appear on a repositioning leg that was sold as a ferry. Anyone asks Tony to make the go/no-go, fuel, route, or release call. A shop asks AMG to bill the owner for fuel or hotels and pass it through.

**The single tell:** money offered to AMG by anyone who is not the aircraft owner. That is the bright line, and it is bright because it is the same line the FAA and the insurer will look at.

**The script when it comes up, verbatim:** "AMG doesn't provide aircraft. I can refer you to a Part 135 operator and I take no fee for that referral." No fee, ever — a fee makes AMG an air charter broker under 14 CFR Part 295. Say the second sentence even when nobody asks about the fee. It is the sentence that protects the certificate.

## The 90-day scoreboard

Five numbers, updated weekly, on one page:

1. Qualified conversations, cumulative.
2. Signed agreements.
3. **Coordination revenue** — fees only, Tony's flying excluded.
4. **Contract pilot revenue** — Tony's own flying, separately.
5. Jobs launched with a complete insurance gate file, as a fraction of jobs launched. This one is 100% or AMG has a problem that no amount of revenue fixes.

### Who this is

AMG Aviation Group is run by Antonio "Tony" Gonzalez, a working corporate pilot. AMG sources and coordinates qualified contract pilots for aircraft owners who need a seat filled and do not have one. That is the whole business.

AMG is not an air carrier, not a charter operator, not a Part 135 certificate holder, not a management company, and not an air charter broker. **AMG never furnishes the aircraft.** AMG supplies coordination and, on request, a pilot's labor. The owner supplies the airplane.

### What AMG does

1. **Temporary and contract pilot sourcing** — callout coverage, vacation, medical, recurrent training.
2. **Maintenance ferry and repositioning coordination** — getting the airplane to the shop or home from it.
3. **Second-pilot, mentor-pilot and insurance-required-pilot coordination** — when the policy names a requirement the owner cannot fill in-house.
4. **Short-term flight department overflow** — covering a department that is one pilot short for a defined period.

### What it costs

| | Days 1–2 | Each additional day |
|---|---|---|
| Piston | $495 | +$150 |
| Turboprop and light jet | $895 | +$250 |

That is AMG's coordination fee. It is one line on one invoice, and it is the only thing AMG charges. On multi-day coverage the fee is soft-capped at 25–30% of total pilot spend.

No markup on anything. No vendor rebates. No percentage of the pilot's rate. On a short one-day ferry the fee is a high share of pilot spend and it does not shrink because the leg is short — most of the work happens before the airplane moves.

### What happens to your customer

**The owner contracts with AMG.** Always — even when a shop or a broker brings the introduction. The shop is a referral source, never the customer, and never the payer.

**The owner pays the pilot directly.** AMG does not hold, route, advance, or rebill trip funds. AMG does not pay fuel, handling, hangar, or landing fees and does not bill them back. Pilot day rates are the pilot's rates, quoted as a range before anyone commits:

- Piston ferry: $400–600/day
- Turboprop / light jet ferry: $700–1,200/day
- Turboprop trip: $1,200–2,100/day
- Light jet trip: $1,500–2,500/day
- Phenom 300, CJ3+/CJ4, PC-24: quoted individually

If AMG fronts an airline positioning ticket, it is reimbursed at cost as a documented AMG out-of-pocket — never as an hourly or per-leg amount, and never as pilot pay.

### The insurance gate

No pilot launches until all of this is in the file. No exceptions, on any job, for anyone.

1. Commercial or ATP certificate. Never a private pilot — 14 CFR 61.113(a) bars a private pilot from acting as PIC for compensation. This is the item that breaks most often on mentor work.
2. Current medical, ratings, type rating where applicable, and the most recent recurrent completion certificate.
3. Total time, multi-engine, turbine, make-and-model, and 90-day times.
4. For a domestic Part 91 SIC: 61.55(b) currency within the preceding 12 calendar months. Not an SIC type rating — 61.55(a)(3) exempts domestic US operations.
5. W-9 and the pilot's own non-owned aircraft liability certificate of insurance.
6. **Written email from the owner's broker or underwriter naming that specific pilot** as falling within the open pilot warranty or added by endorsement. No email, no flight.
7. On a ferry under a special flight permit: the broker's written acknowledgment of the ferry in the same email.

AMG does not say it "verifies" pilots. AMG confirms certificates, currency and time-in-type, and puts the pilot's qualifications in front of the owner's broker for written approval before the flight. The approval is the product. On turbines, assume no usable open pilot warranty and a 24–72 hour endorsement turnaround. A jet pilot on someone's ramp tomorrow morning is usually not possible, and AMG will say so on the call rather than after you have committed a slot.

### Ferry permits

If the aircraft is out of annual, has an expired inspection, or carries an unairworthy discrepancy, it moves under a special flight permit issued under 14 CFR 21.197. The owner or the owner's A&P/IA submits FAA Form 8130-6 under 21.199; the FSDO or a DAR issues the 8130-7 with operating limitations attached, binding through 91.9(a). **AMG is never the applicant and never the signer.** 21.197(c) continuing authorizations are not available to a Part 91 owner, so every ferry needs its own permit. A copy of the 8130-7 and its limitations goes in the trip file before launch.

### Why this is not a liability for you

**Operational control.** The aircraft owner and the pilot in command retain all operational control at all times. AMG does not select the aircraft, does not make or influence the go/no-go, fuel, route, or release decision, and does not hold out or provide air transportation. AMG provides coordination services and, when engaged separately, pilot services. Nothing in an AMG engagement transfers operational control to AMG.

**For the shop:** you are not the customer, you do not pay, you do not sign, and you are not in the money path. You made an introduction. Any referral fee AMG pays goes to the shop entity against the shop's invoice with a W-9 on file — never to an individual, and never without the employer's knowledge.

**For the owner:** AMG's agreement requires a waiver of subrogation in your carrier's favor of AMG on hull and liability, and every pilot is approved in writing by your broker before the flight. Your file is closed before the airplane moves.

An airplane you can't get onto your ramp is a slot you can't bill.

## WORKED EXAMPLE — POST-ANNUAL KING AIR 250 FERRY, KFXE → KAPF

Use this as the calibration reference for filling in the template. Numbers are inside the published bands.

**Proposal No.** 2026-014 · **Date** July 24, 2026 · **Valid through** July 31, 2026
**To:** [Owner entity], Managing Member
**Aircraft:** 2014 Beechcraft King Air 250, N[####], serial BY-###

**1. The requirement as I understood it.** Your King Air 250 has been at [shop] on Fort Lauderdale Executive (KFXE) for its annual inspection. The inspection is complete, the discrepancies are closed, and the aircraft has been returned to service — it is airworthy and no special flight permit is required. You need it back at Naples (KAPF), where it is based, single pilot, no passengers, on Wednesday July 29 or Thursday July 30. Your regular pilot is in recurrent that week. Your policy requires 3,000 hours total, 1,500 multi-engine, and 250 hours in type. If the shop finds anything on the post-inspection run-up that reopens the airworthiness question, this becomes a special flight permit job and I will re-quote the sequence, not the fee.

**2. What AMG will do.** Steps 1–7 as above. Note on step 3: because the aircraft has been returned to service, there is no 8130-6 / 8130-7 in this file. If that changes, you or your IA file it — not AMG.

**3. AMG coordination fee: $895.** Turboprop, days 1–2. One day of work, so no per-day addition. One line, one invoice.

**4. Estimated pilot cost and expenses — PAID BY THE OWNER DIRECTLY.**

| Item | Estimated range | Paid to |
|---|---|---|
| Contract pilot, 1 day, King Air 250 ferry, single pilot | $1,000 – $1,200 | Pilot, directly |
| Ground return KAPF → KFXE (one-way rental or rideshare, ~2 hrs) | $110 – $160 | Vendor, directly |
| Per diem / lodging | $0 (same-day turn) | — |
| **Estimated owner-direct total** | **$1,110 – $1,360** | — |

**Total cost of the move: $2,005 – $2,255**, of which **$895 is payable to AMG** and **$1,110 – $1,360 is payable directly by you to the pilot and vendors.**

Said plainly: on a 45-minute ferry the coordination fee runs roughly 65–80% of pilot spend. That ratio is a function of the leg being short, not of the work being small. The work is finding a 250-current pilot who clears your 3,000/1,500/250 minimums, getting your underwriter to name him in writing, and having that email in the file before he touches the airplane. That happens whether the leg is 45 minutes or four hours. If this were a five-day coverage assignment instead, the fee would be $895 + $250/day for days 3–5 = $1,645, soft-capped at 25–30% of pilot spend.

**5. What AMG needs from you.** Broker contact and COI with waiver of subrogation in AMG's favor; shop's release-to-service confirmation; registration and airworthiness certificate on board; ramp contact at KFXE and hangar access at KAPF; signed agreement.

**6. Next steps.** Signed agreement and broker contact by Friday July 25. Pilot identified and package to your underwriter same day. Written approval expected Monday July 27 — turbine, so assume 24–72 hours. Trip file complete and launch authorized Tuesday July 28. Ferry Wednesday July 29, weather and PIC judgment permitting.

**7 and 8.** Operational control statement, not-an-accepted-assignment line, and acceptance block exactly as in the template.

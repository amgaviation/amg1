## 0. What this document is

Documents 01 through 05 tell you what to say and what to sign. This one tells you what to do, in what order, on what day, and what to write down.

Two rules sit under everything below:

1. **AMG never furnishes the aircraft.** AMG supplies coordination and puts a pilot's qualifications in front of the owner's underwriter. The owner contracts with AMG. The owner pays the pilot directly. AMG's invoice has one line: the coordination fee.
2. **The gate is the product.** The insurance gate in document 02 is not friction slowing down the sale. It is the thing being sold. Every step below either feeds the gate or proves the gate was run.

# PART 1 — THE ENGAGEMENT LIFECYCLE

Thirteen steps. Each one names the actor, the artifact produced, the condition that must be true before the next step starts, and the elapsed time you should actually expect.

Nothing in this runbook authorizes AMG to make an operational decision. Go/no-go, fuel, route, and airworthiness release belong to the PIC and the owner. AMG coordinates around those decisions and never inside them.

---

### STEP 1 — INBOUND REQUEST

**Who:** Tony. Phone, email, or a shop referral.
**Produces:** A row in the tracker (Tab 1), stage `01 Inbound`. Timestamped note of what was asked for, in the caller's words.
**Gate to next step:** The caller has been identified as either the aircraft owner, an owner's representative with authority, or a referral source pointing at an owner.
**Elapsed:** 5–10 minutes.

Capture on the first call, before anything else:

- Who owns the aircraft, and is that person on this call?
- Make and model. Piston, turboprop, or light jet.
- What is the requirement — ferry, callout coverage, second pilot, overflow?
- Where is the airplane now and where does the owner want it?
- Is the aircraft airworthy, or is this out of annual / open discrepancy?
- When does the owner want it moved?

**If the caller is a shop, a broker, a passenger, or a friend of the owner:** they are a referral source, not the customer. Say it on that call. *"I'll need to contract with the owner directly — that's how this works. Can you put me in touch, or give them my number?"* Log the referral source in the tracker and move the row to the owner's name once contact is made.

**If the caller asks AMG to find them an airplane:** stop. *"AMG doesn't provide aircraft. I can refer you to a Part 135 operator and I take no fee for that referral."* Log as `Declined — legality`. A fee on that referral makes AMG an air charter broker under 14 CFR Part 295.

### STEP 2 — QUALIFY

**Who:** Tony, on the same call where possible.
**Produces:** A qualified/declined decision written into the tracker. If qualified, the segment and the requirement type are set.
**Gate to next step:** All four decline categories (Part 2 below) have been run and none of them fired.
**Elapsed:** 10–20 minutes, same call or one callback.

Qualify against four questions, in this order:

1. **Legality.** Is AMG being asked to supply labor, or to supply transportation? Coordination fee, owner-contracted, owner-pays-pilot? Good. All-in delivered price for moving an airplane, price per leg, price per hour, or aircraft-plus-crew as a package? Decline.
2. **Insurance.** Is there a policy in force, is there a named broker, and will the owner authorize AMG to send a pilot's qualifications to that broker for written approval? No broker contact, no job.
3. **Aircraft condition.** Airworthy, or does this need a special flight permit under 14 CFR 21.197? If a permit is needed, the owner or the owner's A&P/IA is the applicant. AMG never applies and never signs.
4. **Safety.** Does the mission as described make sense — weather window, aircraft condition, pilot experience, single-pilot vs. crew, the owner's expectations about timing?

**Time expectation is set here, not later.** On a turbine, assume no usable open pilot warranty. Assume a written endorsement is required and that it takes 24–72 hours. Say so on this call:

> *"On a jet I have to assume your underwriter wants a named endorsement, not the open pilot warranty. That's typically one to three business days from when I hand them the pilot's file. If someone tells you they'll have a jet pilot on your ramp tomorrow morning, ask them who approved him."*

### STEP 3 — SCOPE

**Who:** Tony, with the owner.
**Produces:** A written scope note — services, dates, duration, aircraft category, whether Tony flies it himself, and whether a ferry permit is in play.
**Gate to next step:** The owner agrees on the record to the scope and to the money structure: owner pays the pilot directly, AMG invoices a coordination fee only.
**Elapsed:** Same day. 20–30 minutes.

Scope is where the fee is calculated. Days 1–2 are the base fee.

| Category | Base (days 1–2) | Each additional day | Soft cap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Piston | $495 | +$150 | 25–30% of the owner's total pilot spend |
| Turboprop / light jet | $895 | +$250 | 25–30% of the owner's total pilot spend |

Two known problems, both live, both to be handled out loud rather than hidden:

- **The flat fee breaks above two days.** $895 is a 35% take on a one-day ferry and 3.7% on a fourteen-day coverage block that is far more work. Day-scaling above corrects the top end. Apply the soft cap by hand and write the applied number in the tracker.
- **$495 on a piston is roughly one full piston pilot day** ($400–600/day). That is heavy for the most price-sensitive segment. Where the job is one piston ferry leg and nothing else, expect to discount toward $350–395 or bundle it into a multi-leg scope. Record any discount and the reason — a pattern of discounts is the pricing data.

**Pilot day rates the owner should expect to pay directly** (AMG quotes these as market information, never as AMG's price): piston ferry $400–600; turboprop / light jet ferry $700–1,200; turboprop trip $1,200–2,100; light jet trip $1,500–2,500. Phenom 300, CJ3+/CJ4 and PC-24 are quoted individually.

**If Tony is the PIC on this job:** there is no coordination fee. Pilot services only, under a separate agreement engaging Antonio Gonzalez personally as a contract pilot. Two documents, two roles, and never both fees on one job. Flag the row `Tony-PIC` in the tracker.

**Positioning tickets.** If AMG fronts an airline positioning ticket, it is reimbursed as a documented AMG out-of-pocket, at cost, with the receipt attached. Never as an hourly or per-leg amount and never inside pilot pay.

### STEP 4 — PROPOSAL

**Who:** Tony.
**Produces:** The written proposal (document 03) and the Coordination Services Agreement (document 01), emailed together.
**Gate to next step:** Proposal sent, and it contains the fee, the scope, the four-item money structure, the insurance gate description, and the waiver of subrogation requirement.
**Elapsed:** Sent within 4 business hours of the scope call. Same day, always.

Non-negotiable content:

- **One line for the fee.** A coordination fee for a defined service over defined dates. Never per flight, per leg, per hour, or one all-in delivered price for moving an airplane.
- **The claim language, verbatim:** *"We confirm certificates, currency and time-in-type, and we put the pilot's qualifications in front of your broker for written approval before the flight."* Never *"we verify the pilot."*
- **The waiver of subrogation** in favor of AMG on the owner's hull and liability policy, stated as a standalone, non-negotiable, pre-launch condition. This is the most valuable line in the agreement. It is not a negotiating chip.
- **No guarantees.** No guaranteed availability, no 24/7, no worldwide or nationwide, no network size, no mission counts, no unsubstantiated experience claims. No testimonials until a real customer has given written permission.

Move the row to `04 Proposal Sent` and set a next-action date 2 business days out.

### STEP 5 — ACCEPTANCE

**Who:** The owner signs. Tony countersigns.
**Produces:** Executed Coordination Services Agreement. Owner's broker contact captured. Certificate of insurance requested.
**Gate to next step:** Signature in hand. Not a verbal yes, not a "go ahead and start." Nothing moves on a verbal.
**Elapsed:** 1–5 business days from proposal. Two follow-ups (day 2, day 5), then the row goes to `Dormant`, not `Lost`.

At acceptance, request from the owner in the same email:
- Broker or underwriter name, email, and phone.
- Current COI showing hull and liability, and confirmation the waiver of subrogation in AMG's favor is being endorsed.
- Aircraft make, model and category — for the endorsement request only. AMG does not select or supply the aircraft and does not keep a list of available aircraft.

### STEP 6 — FEE COLLECTED

**Who:** Tony invoices. Owner pays.
**Produces:** Paid invoice, one line, coordination fee. Recorded in tracker as `Actual Collected`.
**Gate to next step:** Coordination fee received before pilot sourcing effort begins in earnest, or on a firm agreed date for established repeat owners.
**Elapsed:** Invoice same day as signature. Payment 0–10 days.

Payment comes from the aircraft owner or the owner's entity. Not from a passenger, not from the shop, not from a broker. If a shop offers to pay AMG's fee on the owner's behalf, decline it and route the invoice back to the owner. This is a hard line, not a preference.

AMG never touches trip funds. Pilot pay, fuel, hangar, landing fees, catering, and handling flow from the owner to the vendor. AMG does not pay operating expenses and rebill them.

**Referral fee out, if one is owed:** paid to the shop entity against the shop's invoice, with a W-9 on file. Never to a Director of Maintenance personally. Fla. Stat. §838.16 makes commercial bribery a third-degree felony and §838.15 catches the recipient. Employer consent is the line. If the DOM does not want his employer to know, there is no referral fee.

### STEP 7 — PILOT SOURCED

**Who:** Tony, working the bench (Tab 2).
**Produces:** A named candidate pilot with a complete qualifications packet.
**Gate to next step:** Pilot has verbally accepted the dates and rate, and has sent every document the gate requires.
**Elapsed:** 2 hours to 2 days. Piston fast, turbine slower, type-specific slowest.

Collect before the pilot's file goes anywhere:

- Commercial or ATP certificate. **Never a private pilot** — 14 CFR 61.113(a) bars a private pilot acting as PIC for compensation. This breaks most often on mentor work, where an owner assumes his high-time friend can be paid to sit right seat. He cannot.
- Medical, ratings, type rating where applicable, and the most recent recurrent completion certificate.
- Total time, multi-engine, turbine, make-and-model, and 90-day times.
- For a domestic Part 91 SIC: 61.55(b) currency within the preceding 12 calendar months. This is **not** an SIC type rating — 61.55(a)(3) exempts domestic US operations. Do not let a broker or an owner talk you into requiring the wrong thing.
- W-9 and the pilot's own non-owned aircraft liability certificate of insurance.

The pilot contracts and invoices the owner. AMG does not employ the pilot, does not pay the pilot, and does not mark up the pilot's rate. There is no markup on anything, and no vendor rebates. Ever.

### STEP 8 — INSURANCE GATE

**Who:** Tony packages. The owner's broker or underwriter decides.
**Produces:** A **written email** from the broker or underwriter naming that specific pilot as within the open pilot warranty or added by endorsement.
**Gate to next step:** **NO EMAIL, NO FLIGHT.** No exceptions, no verbals, no "it'll be fine," no "he's flown it before."
**Elapsed:** Piston with a usable open pilot warranty, same day. Turbine, assume 24–72 hours.

Run document 02 in full. Two additions specific to this step:

- **Turbines: assume no usable open pilot warranty.** Assume a written endorsement is required. Build the schedule around that assumption and tell the owner at Step 2, not here.
- **Ferry under a special flight permit:** the broker's written acknowledgment of the ferry must appear **in the same email**. A pilot approval that does not mention the ferry is not approval for the ferry.

**Permit mechanics.** Out of annual, expired inspection, or an unairworthy discrepancy means a special flight permit under 14 CFR 21.197. The owner or the owner's A&P/IA submits FAA Form 8130-6 under 21.199. The FSDO or a DAR issues the 8130-7 with operating limitations attached. AMG is never the applicant and never the signer.

21.197 itself contains no compensation language — the restrictions come from the operating limitations issued with the permit under 21.199, binding through 91.9(a). Read them. 21.197(c) continuing authorizations are not available to a Part 91 owner, so every AMG ferry needs its own individual permit. The trip file needs a copy of the **8130-7 and its limitations** before launch, not after.

### STEP 9 — OWNER CONFIRMS PILOT

**Who:** The owner.
**Produces:** Written owner acceptance of the named pilot, by email. One line is enough.
**Gate to next step:** Owner has said yes in writing to this specific pilot at this specific rate.
**Elapsed:** Same day as the broker email. Usually under 2 hours.

AMG presents; the owner selects. That distinction matters — it keeps the pilot the owner's contractor and keeps AMG out of operational control. If the owner asks *"which one should I use?"*, give the factual differences in experience and availability and let him choose.

Owner and pilot exchange contact details here. Rate, day count, and payment terms are between them. AMG's fee is already collected and is not part of that conversation.

### STEP 10 — PRE-LAUNCH BRIEF

**Who:** Tony convenes. Owner, pilot, and where relevant the maintenance shop.
**Produces:** A written brief email to all parties, and a complete trip file.
**Gate to next step:** Trip file complete. Every document in hand. Brief acknowledged.
**Elapsed:** 30–45 minutes, the day before departure.

Trip file contents before launch:

- Executed Coordination Services Agreement, and the separate personal contract pilot agreement if Tony is flying.
- Owner COI with the waiver of subrogation in AMG's favor.
- Pilot's certificates, medical, currency, recurrent certificate, time summary, W-9, non-owned COI.
- The broker's written pilot approval email — and its ferry acknowledgment if applicable.
- Owner's written confirmation of the pilot.
- Copy of the 8130-7 and its operating limitations, if a permit ferry.
- Paid coordination fee invoice.
- The brief email itself.

The brief states who is doing what, and states plainly what AMG is **not** doing:

> *"To be explicit about roles: the PIC has sole authority for go/no-go, route, fuel, and acceptance of the aircraft. The owner and his mechanic control airworthiness and release. AMG coordinates schedule and paperwork and has no operational authority over this flight."*

Do not use the word *dispatch* for anything AMG does. Not in the brief, not on the phone, not in a subject line.

---

### STEP 11 — MISSION

**Who:** The pilot flies. The owner owns the decisions.
**Produces:** Timestamped contact log.
**Gate to next step:** Aircraft arrived, pilot released, no open safety item.
**Elapsed:** The length of the job.

AMG's role during the mission is narrow and stays narrow:

- Confirm the pilot is en route to the airplane.
- Confirm departure and arrival, after the fact.
- Handle non-operational logistics — hangar access, gate codes, key handoff, ground transport, who to call at the FBO.
- Relay information between owner, shop, and pilot.

AMG does **not** advise on weather, does not recommend a departure time, does not comment on fuel loads or routing, and does not tell a pilot the owner needs him to go. If the pilot cancels, the answer is *"understood, that's your call — I'll let the owner know and we'll rebuild the schedule."* Then log it and do exactly that.

If a pilot asks AMG to make the call, AMG does not make the call.

Recording note: Florida is a two-party consent state under Fla. Stat. §934.03. Do not record a call without everyone's consent on the recording.

### STEP 12 — CLOSEOUT

**Who:** Tony.
**Produces:** Closed trip file (Part 3 below) and a delivered-job row in Tab 3.
**Gate to next step:** File complete and stored. Nothing pending on money.
**Elapsed:** Within 48 hours of mission completion. Block it on the calendar; this is the step that gets skipped.

Confirm the owner paid the pilot. AMG has no financial interest in that transaction but has a reputational one — a pilot who does not get paid does not come back to the bench.

### STEP 13 — FOLLOW-UP

**Who:** Tony.
**Produces:** Follow-up contact at day 3, day 30, and day 90. Referral-source thank-you.
**Gate to next step:** None — this is the loop back into Step 1.
**Elapsed:** Ongoing.

- **Day 3:** Short email to the owner. Did the pilot perform. Anything AMG should do differently. No selling.
- **Day 3:** Separate note to the referring shop, if any. *"Airplane's back on your ramp and the owner is squared away."* Throughput is the pitch — an airplane a shop cannot get onto its ramp is a slot the shop cannot bill.
- **Day 30:** Check whether the owner has upcoming recurrent, vacation, or medical downtime. This is where callout coverage comes from.
- **Day 90:** Re-contact. Move the row back to `01 Inbound` when a real requirement appears.

| Path | Realistic total elapsed |

# PART 2 — THE DECLINE PATH

**Declining early is a feature.** A job declined on the first call costs twenty minutes. The same job declined at Step 10 costs a week of sourcing, a burned pilot relationship, and an owner who now thinks AMG wasted his time. The worst version costs a denied hull claim and a subrogation demand.

Tony declines jobs. That is the point. Say so plainly to owners — it is why the ones who stay are worth having.

### The four reason categories

**1. INSURANCE — the underwriter said no, or there is no underwriter to ask.**

Fires when: no policy in force; the owner will not give up broker contact; the broker declines the pilot; the owner refuses the waiver of subrogation; a permit ferry the underwriter will not acknowledge in writing.

Decline language:

> *"Your underwriter won't put this pilot on your policy in writing, so we don't have a flight. I'm not going to launch a pilot your insurer hasn't approved — if anything happened, your hull claim is the thing that gets denied. Let's find a pilot your carrier will approve, or let's get you a name from your broker and I'll work with that."*

Never launch on a verbal from a broker's assistant. Never launch on "the open pilot warranty probably covers it." Probably is not a document.

**2. SAFETY — the mission as described does not work.**

Fires when: the schedule only closes by pressing weather; the pilot is legal but thin for the airplane, the route, or the conditions; single-pilot in an aircraft that should be crewed; an owner who wants to be talked out of a no-go.

Decline language:

> *"I can source the pilot. I can't build you a schedule that only works if the weather cooperates. If we set it up so the only way to make the date is to push, the pilot is going to feel that from your side and from mine. Give me a two-day window instead of a hard date and I'll take the job."*

Note the shape: AMG is declining **the schedule**, not making the go/no-go call. AMG does not make go/no-go decisions. AMG declines to sell a schedule that manufactures pressure.

**3. AIRCRAFT CONDITION — the airplane is not in a state to move legally.**

Fires when: out of annual with no permit path; an open discrepancy nobody will sign off; the owner wants to move it "just this once"; the shop and the owner disagree about whether it is airworthy; a permit is required and nobody on the owner's side will be the applicant.

Decline language:

> *"That airplane needs a special flight permit before it goes anywhere. Your A&P or IA files the 8130-6 — I can't be the applicant and I won't sign it. Once the FSDO or a DAR issues the 8130-7 and you send me a copy with the operating limitations attached, I'll put a pilot in front of your underwriter. Until then there's nothing for me to coordinate."*

If the owner and the shop disagree about airworthiness, AMG does not arbitrate and does not pick a side. That is the mechanic's determination and the PIC's acceptance.

**4. LEGALITY — the structure asked for is not one AMG can be part of.**

Fires when: the caller wants AMG to find them an airplane; the caller wants one all-in price to move an airplane; a passenger, shop, or broker wants to be the paying party; the caller wants a price per leg or per hour; a dry lease is proposed with AMG-supplied crew as a condition; the caller wants AMG to hold trip funds and rebill expenses.

Decline language:

> *"What you're describing is charter, and AMG isn't a charter operator — we don't provide aircraft. I can refer you to a Part 135 operator and I take no fee for that referral. If you own the airplane and you need a qualified pilot for it, that's the conversation I want to have."*

On the sham dry lease specifically: a dry lease that requires the lessee to use AMG's crew is a wet lease wearing a costume, and it is the pattern FAA enforcement looks for first. AMG does not participate, does not draft it, and does not introduce the parties for it.

### How to decline well

1. **Decline on the call it becomes clear.** Do not go away and think about it. Do not send a proposal you intend to price out of reach.
2. **Name the category out loud.** *"This is an insurance problem"* or *"this is a legality problem."* Owners respect a stated reason and resent a vague one.
3. **Give the path back in, if there is one.** Most declines are conditional: get the permit, get the endorsement, widen the window. Say what would change the answer.
4. **Hand off where you can, and take nothing for it.** Part 135 referral: no fee. Shop referral: no fee. Another contract pilot outside the bench: no fee. Being useful with nothing attached is the cheapest marketing AMG has.
5. **Log it.** Stage `Declined`, reason category in its own column. Four declines for the same reason in a month is a signal about which market AMG is calling into.
6. **Do not re-litigate.** One clear decline, then move on. If the owner comes back with the condition fixed, the row reopens at `01 Inbound`.

# PART 3 — THE CLOSEOUT FILE AND THE PROOF NOTE

## 3.1 The closeout file (retained, private, never published)

One folder per job. Naming: `YYYY-MM-DD_JobNNN_LastName`. Retain **seven years** — beyond any plausible statute of limitations on a hull claim or a subrogation action.

Contents:

1. Executed Coordination Services Agreement (plus the separate personal contract pilot agreement if Tony flew).
2. Owner COI showing hull and liability, with the waiver of subrogation in AMG's favor.
3. Pilot packet: certificates, medical, ratings, type rating, recurrent completion certificate, 61.55(b) currency where applicable, time summary (TT/ME/turbine/make-and-model/90-day), W-9, non-owned aircraft liability COI.
4. **The broker's written pilot approval email, complete with headers and timestamp.** If nothing else survives, this survives.
5. Ferry acknowledgment from the broker, where applicable — same email.
6. 8130-7 and its operating limitations, where applicable.
7. Owner's written confirmation of the pilot.
8. Pre-launch brief email and acknowledgments.
9. Coordination fee invoice, one line, and proof of payment.
10. Receipts for any AMG out-of-pocket positioning ticket, at cost.
11. Contact log: date, time, who, what was said.
12. Any decline or safety discussion that occurred mid-job.

Store the file so it can be produced whole, in order, within one business day of a request from an insurer or a lawyer. A file you can produce in a day is a file that makes AMG a witness. A file you have to reconstruct is a file that makes AMG a defendant.

## 3.2 The proof note (one page, non-identifying, publishable)

Written within 48 hours of closeout, while the detail is fresh. This is the first case study — and it exists because AMG cannot use testimonials yet.

**MAY appear:**
- Aircraft **category and class only**: "a light jet," "a cabin-class piston twin," "a single-engine turboprop."
- Region at the level of a metro area or broader: "South Florida," "the Southeast."
- The requirement in generic terms: "out of annual, needed to move to a maintenance facility."
- Elapsed time from first call to broker approval, and from approval to launch.
- What the gate actually caught, described generically: "the first candidate wasn't within the open pilot warranty; the second was added by endorsement in 41 hours."
- The process steps and their sequence.
- AMG's fee structure, stated generally.

**MAY NOT appear:**
- Tail numbers, serial numbers, N-number fragments, or registration photos.
- Owner names, entity names, LLC names, or anything traceable to a registry lookup.
- Pilot names.
- Shop or FBO names.
- Origin and destination airports, routing, dates, or times specific enough to reconstruct the flight. Do not write "KFXE to KPBI on the 14th" — a category and a region only.
- Anything that lets a reader with a registry and a flight-tracking site identify the airplane. **Test every note against that: category + date + city pair is often enough to identify an aircraft. Category + region + no date is not.**
- Photographs of the aircraft, the panel, the ramp, or paperwork.
- Dollar amounts the owner paid a pilot.

**Format — one page, five short blocks:**

1. **Situation** (2–3 sentences). Category of aircraft, generic requirement, generic constraint.
2. **What AMG did** (4–6 bullets). Sourcing, the gate, the broker submission, the permit path if any, the brief.
3. **What the gate caught** (2–3 sentences). This is the whole value of the note. If nothing was caught, say what was confirmed instead.
4. **Timeline** (3–4 lines, elapsed hours only, no calendar dates).
5. **Structure** (2 sentences). Owner contracted with AMG; owner paid the pilot directly; AMG invoiced one coordination fee. Restate it every time — it trains the market.

**Claims discipline in the note.** Never *"we verify the pilot."* Always *"we confirm certificates, currency and time-in-type, and we put the pilot's qualifications in front of the owner's broker for written approval before the flight."* No guaranteed availability, no 24/7, no worldwide or nationwide, no network size, no mission counts, no years of experience that cannot be substantiated. One job is one job — write "a recent engagement," not "we routinely."

**Testimonials.** A proof note is AMG's own description of its own work. A **testimonial** is a customer's words, and it requires **written permission from that customer before use** — FTC rules at 16 CFR Part 465, with civil penalties running to roughly $53,088 per violation. Keep the permission email in the closeout file. If AMG ever gives anything of value in exchange for a testimonial — a discount, a free coordination, anything — that incentive must be **clearly and conspicuously disclosed** alongside the testimonial. The simpler policy, and the one AMG runs by default: do not solicit testimonials, do not pay for them, and publish nothing in a customer's voice until written permission is in hand.

# PART 4 — THE PIPELINE TRACKER (GOOGLE SHEETS SPEC)

## 4.1 Why a spreadsheet and not a CRM

At this volume a CRM is a tax. It costs $30–100/month, it takes a weekend to configure, it invents fields nobody fills in, and — the real problem — it puts a screen between Tony and the phone. Every hour spent building pipeline automation is an hour not spent at a shop counter at 07:45. AMG has one operator. A spreadsheet is faster to change than a CRM is to configure, and every column below exists because a decision depends on it.

**The trigger to move.** Switch to a real CRM when **either** of these is true:

- **250 active contacts** in Tab 1 — the point at which finding a row by scrolling stops working and manual follow-up dates start getting missed.
- **15 delivered jobs per month** in Tab 3 — the point at which repeat-customer history, per-job document status, and follow-up scheduling stop fitting in one row each.

Below those numbers, a spreadsheet wins. Above them, the spreadsheet starts costing jobs. Do not move early because a CRM feels more like a real business. Revenue is what makes it a real business.

**Whichever tool holds this data, it holds owner names, phone numbers, and aircraft. Treat it as confidential. No sharing links, no public sheets, no exports to a personal address.**

# PART 5 — THE DAILY AND WEEKLY OPERATING RHYTHM

Tony is a working corporate pilot. The schedule below is what a full weekday on the ground looks like. On a trip day, protect the 15:00–16:00 block and let the rest go.

## 5.1 The weekday

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 07:00–07:45 | Prep | Open the `TODAY` filter view. Read overdue and due-today rows. Write the call list on paper — 12 to 18 names. Re-read notes on the top five. |
| **07:45–08:45** | **PHONE BLOCK 1** | Shop counters are staffed from 07:00 and the morning rush is over by 07:45. This is when a DOM will actually pick up. Manual dial only. Log every call before the next one. |
| 08:45–09:15 | Proposal work | Anything promised on the morning calls goes out now, while the conversation is still in your head. |
| 09:15–10:00 | Insurance gate work | Broker submissions, follow-ups on pending endorsements, chasing pilot documents. Underwriters answer email in the morning. |
| 10:00–13:30 | **RAMP** | Field of the day. Walk the shops, the FBO counters, the transient ramp. Face to face. Card, one-pager, one question, out. Notes go into the phone before starting the car. |
| 13:30–14:15 | Lunch / tracker | Enter every ramp contact into Tab 1 with a next action and a date. Rows entered in the car do not get entered at all. |
| 14:15–15:00 | Pilot bench | Bench calls, new pilot intake, document collection, expiry chasing. Tab 2 conditional formatting is the to-do list. |
| **15:00–16:00** | **PHONE BLOCK 2** | The DOM is back at a desk closing work orders. Second-touch calls, callbacks, and the names missed in Block 1. |
| 16:00–17:00 | Admin | Invoices, closeout files, proof note if a job just closed, tomorrow's `Next Action Date` set on every row touched today. |
| 17:00 | Stop | Update the scorecard row. Close it. |

**Phone rules, non-negotiable:**

- **Manual dial only.** No auto-dialer, no predictive dialer, no bulk SMS, no ringless voicemail. Not for "just this list."
- **Florida is two-party consent** (Fla. Stat. §934.03). Do not record a call unless everyone on it has consented on the recording.
- **Maintain a written internal DNC policy** and honor requests immediately, regardless of B2B status. 47 CFR 64.1200(d) requires the written policy. A DNC request goes into Tab 1 as `Do Not Contact` in Notes and the row moves to `Lost` the same hour.
- **Florida Telemarketing Act (§§501.601–501.626).** A "commercial telephone seller" needs an FDACS license under §501.605 or a filed exemption affidavit under §501.604. The B2B exemption at §501.604(10) does **not** cover AMG — it requires three years operating under the same name. The exemption that plausibly fits is §501.604(3): no major sales presentation by phone, with the sale completed at a later face-to-face meeting. **That is precisely why the ramp visit is in the middle of the day and the phone block is short.** The call gets a meeting; the meeting gets the sale. **CONFIRM THIS WITH FDACS IN WRITING BEFORE SCALING CALL VOLUME** and put the response in the corporate file.

## 5.2 The five-field-day rotation

Seven fields, five weekdays. Two of the days pair a smaller field with a larger one. Rotate the pairs quarterly so no field goes more than two weeks without a visit.

| Day | Field(s) | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Monday** | **FXE** — Fort Lauderdale Executive | The densest maintenance and light-jet population in the service area. Home field advantage. Start the week where the volume is. |
| **Tuesday** | **OPF** — Miami-Opa Locka Executive | Turbine and jet heavy, large MRO presence, transient jet traffic. The best turbine-lead field. |
| **Wednesday** | **TMB + PMP** — Miami Executive + Pompano Beach Airpark | Piston and light twin density. Flight schools, owner-flown singles and twins, small independent shops. High volume of small jobs. |
| **Thursday** | **PBI + LNA** — Palm Beach International + Palm Beach County Park (Lantana) | PBI for corporate flight departments and overflow work; LNA for the piston and warbird community ten minutes away. Pair them in one trip north. |
| **Friday** | **BCT** — Boca Raton | Owner-flown turboprops and light jets, strong owner-operator community. Friday afternoons are social on that ramp; the conversations run longer. |

Log every field day in Tab 1 with `Source = Ramp Visit` and `Source Detail =` the field identifier. After a quarter, the visit-to-contact and contact-to-job ratios per field tell you which two days to double and which one to drop. Do not guess at that — the tracker answers it.

## 5.3 The week

- **Monday 07:00.** Set the week's targets against the scorecard. Review every row in `04 Proposal Sent` and `08 At Insurance Gate` — those two stages are where deals die of silence.
- **Wednesday 16:00.** Mid-week check. If calls are below half the weekly target, Thursday's ramp block gets cut and moved to the phone.

- **Monthly, first business day.** Reprice review: read `Fee realization` and `Avg take rate`. If piston realization is under 0.90 or turbine take rate is over 0.30, change the price sheet rather than continuing to discount case by case.

# PART 6 — THE WEEKLY SCORECARD

Six numbers. Nothing else goes on this card. Enter them Friday at 16:00 on a fourth tab named `SCORECARD` with one row per week.

**Definitions — write them down so the numbers stay honest:**

1. **Calls** — manually dialed outbound attempts to a named person. Voicemails count. Redials to the same person on the same day count once.
2. **Conversations** — a live two-way exchange with a decision-maker or a real referral path, lasting long enough to learn something worth logging. A receptionist is not a conversation.
3. **Proposals** — written proposals sent, with a fee, to a named aircraft owner. Verbal quotes do not count.
4. **Closes** — signed Coordination Services Agreements. Not verbal yes. Not "send me the paperwork."
5. **Cash collected** — coordination fees actually received that week, from Tab 1 column P. Not invoiced. Received.
6. **Pilots added** — pilots added to Tab 2 with `Docs Complete = COMPLETE`. An incomplete pilot is not bench.

**Targets, weeks 1–4:**

| | Wk 1 | Wk 2 | Wk 3 | Wk 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Calls | 40 | 60 | 75 | 75 |
| Conversations | 10 | 16 | 22 | 22 |
| Proposals | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| Closes | 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| Cash collected | $0 | $0 | $495–895 | $895–1,500 |
| Pilots added (complete) | 3 | 3 | 2 | 2 |

**How to read the card:**

- **Weeks 1 and 2 are supposed to show zero closes.** The sales cycle on a first-time owner runs two to four weeks and the insurance gate adds 24–72 hours on turbines. A close in week 1 is luck, not a trend. Zero closes with the call and conversation targets met is a week that worked.
- **Bench first.** Ten complete pilots by the end of week 4 is the real week-4 deliverable. Cash follows bench, because a job AMG cannot staff is a job AMG cannot sell.
- **Diagnose in order.** Calls low → the schedule slipped; protect the two phone blocks. Calls fine but conversations low → wrong times or wrong list; move calls into the 07:45 and 15:00 windows and toward shop counters. Conversations fine but proposals low → the qualify step is soft or the ask is missing. Proposals fine but closes low → check whether the waiver of subrogation or the owner-pays-pilot structure is where deals stall, and read documents 01 and 04 again.
- **Declines are not failures.** A week with four declines and one close is a better week than five closes that should have been declined. Track the decline reasons alongside the six numbers and read them monthly.
- **From week 5 on**, hold 75 calls / 22 conversations / 4 proposals as the steady state and let closes and cash rise as the pipeline fills. Re-baseline the targets at week 12 against actual data, not against this table.

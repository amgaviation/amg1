## How to read this list

**VERIFIED** — the entry was taken from the company's own website or from a primary
industry directory (IADA accredited-dealer directory). Address, phone and scope of work
came off the source, not off a third-party aggregator.

**UNVERIFIED** — the entry came from a secondary source (search summary, airport
directory, trade listing). The company is real, but confirm the number and the contact
name before you burn a first impression on it.

**FLAG** — something about the entry is off-profile for AMG's first sale: FBO-integrated,
charter-affiliated, jet-only, dual-hat as a dealer, or a bench shop that receives parts
rather than airplanes. Flags are not automatic skips. They tell you what the call is
actually for.

Two hard rules that do not bend on any of these calls:

1. The **owner** is AMG's contracting party. A shop is a referral source. A broker is a
   referral source. A dealer is a referral source. None of them is the customer, none of
   them pays AMG, and none of them appears on AMG's invoice.
2. AMG does not provide aircraft. If any of these contacts asks AMG to find a plane, the
   answer is the referral to a Part 135 operator with **no fee taken**.

Do not build a call list out of Controller.com or Trade-A-Plane listings. Their terms
prohibit aggregating and reusing listing data. Read listings there to see who is moving
metal, then call that dealer directly off their own website.

## Segment 2 — Florida aviation insurance brokers

**This is the highest-leverage segment and it should not be treated as a sales call.**
Underwriters create AMG's demand. A policy that requires a mentor pilot for the first 25
hours turns a newly-typed turbine owner into an AMG customer on day one, and the broker is
the person who reads that requirement out loud to the owner.

The ask on these calls is narrow and it is a favor to them, not from them:

> "When an underwriter writes an open pilot warranty your insured can't meet, or a mentor
> requirement, or a dual requirement after a step-up, you're the one who has to tell them.
> I supply the pilot. I don't supply the airplane and I never touch trip funds. I'd like
> you to have my number for that conversation."

Say the claims language exactly as written in the operating facts. **Never say AMG
"verifies" pilots** — that creates a duty of care AMG is not insured for. Say: *we confirm
certificates, currency and time-in-type, and we put the pilot's qualifications in front of
your broker for written approval before the flight.*

Every one of these calls should also end with AMG's own ask: a **waiver of subrogation in
favor of AMG** on the owner's hull and liability policy is a standalone, non-negotiable,
pre-launch condition in AMG's agreement. Brokers will tell you fast whether their
carriers will endorse it. That intelligence is worth more than the lead.

## Calling order

The sequence is built on one idea: **call the people who create demand before you call the
people who consume it.** A shop can only give you the airplane in front of it today. A
broker and an underwriter can put a mentor-pilot requirement into a policy that generates
recurring work for a year.

**Tier 1 — Brokers (highest leverage, no price conversation).**
Aviation Assurance → Sunset Aviation Insurance → Alexander Aviation Associates →
S.T. Good (drive to Stuart, don't phone it).
These calls have no close and no quote. The output is: the broker knows what AMG does,
knows AMG never furnishes an aircraft, and knows whether their carriers will endorse a
waiver of subrogation. Do these first because they are the calls where AMG has the most
credibility and the least to sell.

**Tier 2 — On-profile piston and turboprop shops (fastest first revenue).**
Premier Aircraft Service (KFXE, home field) → Double M Aviation (KLAL) →
Airplane Intel / MX On-Demand (KOCF) → AeroMX (KSRQ) →
Sarasota Avionics & Maintenance (KVNC) → Daytona Aircraft Services (KDAB).
These shops touch airplanes with realistic open pilot warranties, $400-1,200/day pilots,
and permits that move in days rather than weeks. This is where the first sale comes from.

**Tier 3 — Jet shops (highest value per mission, slowest to close).**
Boca Aircraft Maintenance → Reliable Jet Maintenance → South Florida Jet Center →
Florida Jet.
On turbines assume there is **no usable open pilot warranty** and that a written
endorsement takes 24-72 hours. Never promise "someone there tomorrow morning" on a jet.
Sell the approval step as the product: the reason AMG exists is that the endorsement email
arrives before the airplane moves.

**Tier 4 — Dealers.**
Southern Cross → 5X5 Trading → Global Wings → Aero Ventures → Global Aircraft Corp →
(Banyan and Flightline last, sales desk only).
Transaction-driven, so the timing is theirs, not yours. Worth a call each and a standing
introduction, but do not let this segment eat a morning.

**Tier 5 — Unverified entries.**
Spectrum Aero, AERA AvGroup, and the four unverified brokers. Confirm first, then slot
them into the tier they belong in.

### Daily sequence

**Open every day with Tier 1 or Tier 2 — never with Tier 3 or 4.**

- **First block of the morning (08:00-10:00): shops.** Directors of Maintenance are on the
  floor and reachable early. By mid-morning they are under an airplane. This is the block
  that produces the first sale.
- **Late morning (10:30-12:00): brokers.** Agency offices are staffed and not yet in the
  afternoon renewal crush. Broker calls are relationship calls and do better when nobody
  is holding a wrench.
- **Afternoon: dealers, follow-ups, and verification of Tier 5.** Dealers are the most
  interruptible segment and the least time-sensitive.
- **Never** open a day on the jet shops. They are a second-half-of-the-week call, once
  you've said the pitch enough times that morning to have it clean.

### Compliance notes that apply to every call on this list

- **Manual dial only.** No auto-dialer, no bulk SMS, no ringless voicemail.
- **Florida is two-party consent** for recording (Fla. Stat. §934.03). Do not record a
  call without saying so and getting agreement.
- **Florida Telemarketing Act** (§§501.601-501.626): the B2B exemption at §501.604(10)
  does **not** cover AMG — it requires three years operating under the same name. The
  exemption that plausibly fits is §501.604(3): no major sales presentation over the
  phone, sale completed at a later face-to-face meeting. Structure these calls that way —
  the phone call sets a meeting, the meeting makes the sale. **Confirm the exemption with
  FDACS before running the list.**
- Maintain a written do-not-call policy (47 CFR 64.1200(d)) regardless of B2B status. Log
  every request to stop calling, on this list, the day it happens.
- **Referral fees, if AMG ever pays one:** pay the **shop entity** against its invoice with
  a W-9 on file. Never a Director of Maintenance personally. Fla. Stat. §838.16 makes
  commercial bribery a third-degree felony, and §838.15 catches the recipient. Employer
  consent is the line.
- No testimonials from anyone on this list until they give written permission (FTC 16 CFR
  Part 465). No claims of guaranteed availability, 24/7, nationwide coverage, network size,
  or mission counts.

## 0. Why this document exists

Everything written so far is demand-side. It assumes that when an owner says yes, there is a qualified pilot who can be at the airplane. There isn't one yet.

Selling a ferry with no pilot to fly it is worse than not selling it. A ferry AMG cannot staff costs the shop a slot, costs the owner a week, and costs AMG the referral source permanently — in a market this small, once. The bench is the product. The coordination fee is what an owner pays for a qualified pilot being *findable* on the day he calls, and for that pilot's file reaching his underwriter complete and first time. Approval itself is always trip-specific and always after the call — that is what the gate in `02` is. The bench is what makes the gate fast, not what lets it be skipped.

Second reason this document exists: the published day-rate bands in the pricing work are blocked on twelve written rate confirmations from real pilots. Those confirmations come out of recruiting. Section 6 is how they get collected.

### What AMG is offering a pilot — say this the same way every time

AMG coordinates contract pilot work for Part 91 aircraft owners. The owner contracts with AMG, the owner pays the pilot directly, and AMG's fee is billed to the owner separately as a coordination fee. AMG takes no cut of pilot pay, does not mark up the pilot's rate, and never touches trip funds.

Two things follow from that, and both must be said out loud in recruiting:

1. **AMG is not the paying party.** AMG cannot float a pilot, cannot advance against an owner's invoice, and cannot guarantee the owner pays. What AMG does is make the payment terms explicit in writing before the pilot accepts, put the pilot in direct contact with the party holding the money, and stop the job from launching until the paperwork is real.
2. **AMG does not put a pilot in front of an airplane the insurer has not approved.** No written email from the owner's broker or underwriter naming that specific pilot, no flight. That is a hard stop and it protects the pilot more than it protects AMG.

Do not oversell either point. Pilots have heard the pitch before.

## 1. Where the pilots actually are

Order below is roughly by yield per hour of effort for the first 90 days. Nothing here is a mass campaign — manual outreach only, one at a time. No auto-dialer, no bulk SMS, no ringless voicemail (see `02` and the telemarketing constraints; the same discipline applies to pilot-side outreach even though it is not a sale).

### 1.1 Facebook groups — the highest-yield channel, and the one most likely to get you banned

Named groups that actually carry this traffic: **Ferry Pilots**, **Ferry Pilot Network**, **Contract Pilots**, **Contract Pilot Jobs**, **Corporate Pilot Jobs / Corporate Aviation Jobs**, **Aircraft Ferry Flights**, and the regional ones — **Florida Pilots**, **South Florida Aviation**. Also worth joining: **Aircraft Owners and Pilots** general groups, where owners and the pilots who fly for them mix.

Group names and admin rules shift. Before posting anywhere: read the pinned rules, then read the last thirty posts to see what a legitimate post looks like in that room. Several of these groups ban job posts outright, several restrict them to a weekly thread, and several have an admin who will remove a business post on sight and never let you back in.

**How to approach.** Join, then wait. Answer three or four technical questions in the group before you ever post an opportunity — a fuel-planning question, a permit question, a "what does the 8130-7 limitation actually say" question. Post the recruiting message (Section 3) as a working pilot who has coverage to fill, not as a company that is hiring. Then move every serious reply to DM, and every serious DM to a phone call.

**What the pilot wants to hear.** Real airplane, real dates, real money, terms in writing before he says yes, and that you are a pilot too. In these groups the fastest credibility move is naming what you fly and where you are based.

### 1.2 Barnstormers — "Ferry Service" and pilot-services classifieds

Barnstormers is where working ferry pilots advertise themselves. The **Ferry Service** category is a directory of exactly the people AMG needs, with contact details published voluntarily.

**How to approach.** Do not post an ad first. Read the listings and contact people individually — the listing tells you their equipment, their region, and often their rate posture. Email or call, reference their listing specifically, and lead with a concrete class of work ("piston singles and light twins out of shops in South Florida, ferry and reposition"). A listing that has been up for months is a pilot with capacity.

**What the pilot wants to hear.** That you found his listing and read it. That the work is recurring rather than a one-off. That he is not being asked to bid against five other people for a $500 day.

### 1.3 Trade-A-Plane — services listings

Same logic, older and more owner-facing audience. The services section carries ferry pilots, and it also carries shops — which is a demand-side lead and a supply-side lead in the same page. Contact individually. Treat any shop you find here as a referral source per `04`, never as the customer.

### 1.4 Pilots of America and the general forums

Pilots of America (`pilotsofamerica.com`) has an active flying-for-hire and careers section and a heavy GA and light-twin population. Purple Board / airline-adjacent boards are less useful for Part 91 piston work but useful for the retired-airline segment.

**How to approach.** Forums punish sales posts harder than Facebook does. Build a post history first. Then a plainly-labeled post: what the work is, what region, what it pays, what qualifications gate it. Be explicit that private pilots cannot be paid — you will get fewer, better replies.

**What the pilot wants to hear.** Specificity. Forums are full of vague "opportunities." A post that names the equipment class, the geography, and the insurance requirement reads as real.

### 1.5 Type-club forums — the highest-quality, lowest-volume channel

For make-and-model time, the type clubs are the only efficient source: **American Bonanza Society**, **Cessna Flyer / Cessna Pilots Association**, **Cirrus Owners and Pilots Association (COPA)**, **Malibu M-Class Owners and Pilots Association (MMOPA)**, **Twin Cessna Flyer**, **King Air / turboprop owner groups**, **TBM Owners and Pilots Association**, **PC-12 owner groups**.

These matter because the insurance gate is usually decided on **make-and-model time**, not total time. A named PA-46 or King Air mentor pilot with 800 hours in type clears an underwriter that a 5,000-hour generalist will not.

**How to approach.** Join as a member where AMG or Tony qualifies. Do not spam. Identify the two or three people in each club who already do mentor and transition work — they are visible, they are named repeatedly in threads, and they already understand insurance-mandated dual. Contact them directly.

**What the pilot wants to hear.** That the insurance approval is being handled in writing before the trip, because mentor pilots get burned on exactly this — showing up to find the owner's policy never actually covered them, or discovering the "mentor" flight was expected to be flown by someone who does not hold a commercial certificate.

### 1.6 Local CFI and A&P networks — South Florida ramp-level

FXE (Fort Lauderdale Executive), PMP (Pompano Beach), BCT (Boca Raton), TMB (Miami Executive), OPF (Opa-locka), FLL, PBI, SUA (Stuart), APF (Naples), LAL (Lakeland). Walk the FBOs and the maintenance hangars. The A&P/IA who signs the ferry-permit application knows which pilots his shop trusts to move an airplane — that is a two-for-one contact.

**How to approach.** In person, mid-morning, mid-week. Ask who they call now when a customer's airplane needs to move and the customer can't fly it. Take the name. Call the name.

**What the pilot wants to hear (and the shop too).** That AMG is not competing with them and is not a broker taking a cut of their work.

### 1.7 Flight schools with time-building instructors

Every 141 and large 61 school in South Florida has instructors between 800 and 1,500 hours waiting on an airline call. They are commercially certificated, current, cheap relative to a corporate pilot, and hungry for multi and cross-country time. They are a legitimate fit for **piston ferry only**.

**How to approach.** Chief flight instructor first, never around him. Frame it as occasional work for his senior instructors that does not compete with the school and does not pull them off the schedule.

**What the pilot wants to hear.** Paid PIC cross-country time in something other than a 172, on a day off. They will accept the low end of the piston band. They will also be the ones an underwriter is most likely to reject on total time — check the gate before promising anything.

### 1.8 Retired and furloughed airline pilots in South Florida

South Florida is dense with retired 121 crews — the post-65 population is large, local, and bored. Many hold current medicals, many still instruct or fly Part 91 for a friend. They are the best source for **turboprop and light jet second-in-command and mentor work**, and they are frequently already known to underwriters.

**How to approach.** Through the type clubs, through the FBOs, through the local chapters and pilot associations, and through referral from the first two you meet. This segment recruits by word of mouth almost exclusively.

**What the pilot wants to hear.** Occasional, structured, low-drama work with the paperwork done in advance. Not a schedule. Not a commitment. They are not trying to have a job again.

### 1.9 Channels to skip for now

Indeed / LinkedIn job posts (wrong audience, produces resume volume, no ferry pilots), paid job boards aimed at 121 hiring, and anything that requires AMG to advertise availability of lift. AMG recruits pilots; AMG never advertises that it can move a particular airplane on a particular day.

## 2. What a contract pilot actually cares about — ranked, from his side of the table

This is the ranking to design the offer around. It is not the ranking a company would guess.

**1. Am I going to get paid, and by whom.**
Every contract pilot has been stiffed or slow-paid. Answer before he asks: the aircraft owner pays you directly, here is the owner's name and the billing contact, here is the rate, here is the term (net 15 / on completion / however the owner's agreement reads), and AMG is not in the middle of that money. AMG's honest position is that the payer is a party the pilot can see and invoice, rather than a middleman who might disappear. Do not dress that up as a benefit it isn't — AMG cannot float, and the pilot should hear that plainly the first time.

**2. Is the paperwork real.**
Is there a written engagement, or is this a handshake that becomes a problem when something goes wrong. What does he sign, who is the counterparty, what is his liability posture, does the owner's policy cover him. A pilot who has been on a trip with no written anything will value this more than an extra hundred dollars a day.

**3. Is the insurance sorted before I show up.**
The specific fear: driving or flying to an airport, meeting the airplane, and finding out he is not named, not approved, or outside the open pilot warranty — and then either flying uncovered or going home unpaid. AMG's answer is the gate in `02`: written email from the owner's broker or underwriter naming that specific pilot before launch, plus the broker's written acknowledgment of the ferry when it goes under a special flight permit. No email, no flight. That rule cuts both ways and the pilot should be told it cuts in his favor.

**4. Do I know the terms before I accept.**
Rate, day count, who pays positioning, who pays the hotel, what happens on a weather day, what happens if the airplane is not ready when he arrives, what happens if the trip cancels. A pilot will accept a modest rate with clear terms over a good rate with fog. Ambiguity is what makes contract work miserable.

**5. Is this person going to waste my day.**
Ferry work is full of jobs that evaporate — the airplane isn't out of annual yet, the permit never got issued, the owner went quiet. Every wasted day is unpaid and unrecoverable. AMG's differentiation is procedural: nothing gets offered to a pilot until the airworthiness path, the permit status, and the insurance approval are actually moving. A hold day policy stated in advance is worth more than a rate increase.

**Below the line, but present:** equipment and time-in-type he wants to log, geography, whether he'll be treated as a professional or as a favor, and whether the work repeats. Repeat work beats rate for most of this population.

### The honest pitch, in one paragraph

> "Terms and insurance approval are settled in writing before you launch. You know the rate, the day count, the payer, and the cancellation terms before you say yes, and you have a written email from the owner's broker naming you before you touch the airplane. AMG doesn't pay you — the owner does, directly — so I'm not going to pretend I can float you. What I can do is make sure you never drive to an airport for a job that was never real."

## 4. The bench record

One record per pilot. Plain and durable — a spreadsheet plus a folder of PDFs is sufficient at this size and is easier to produce in a claim than anything clever. Keep documents; do not just record that you saw them.

The record is **not** a substitute for the insurance gate. The gate in `02-insurance-gate-and-trip-file.md` runs per trip, every trip, in full. The bench record exists to make the gate fast, not to skip it. Nothing in this file may be treated as pre-approval.

### 4.1 Fields

**Identity and contact**
- Legal name as it appears on the certificate; preferred name
- Mobile, email, home base airport, city
- Emergency contact (name, relationship, phone)

**Certificates and medical** — document on file for each
- Certificate level (Commercial / ATP) and number; certificate issue date
- Ratings: ASEL / AMEL / instrument / rotorcraft as applicable
- Type ratings held, with dates
- CFI / CFII / MEI, if any, with expiration
- Medical class, issue date, expiration by class and age
- Flight review date (or equivalent under 61.56)

**Currency**
- Last recurrent training completion — provider, course, aircraft, date, certificate of completion on file
- 61.55(b) SIC currency date per make and model, where the pilot is being used as SIC on domestic Part 91 (see `02`; this is 61.55(b) currency, not an SIC type rating — 61.55(a)(3) exempts domestic US operations)
- 61.57 landing currency, day and night
- IPC date if instrument currency lapsed

**Times** — as of a stated date, always
- Total time; ME; turbine; instrument
- Make-and-model time for each type the pilot is offered on
- Last 90 days total, and last 90 days in type
- PIC vs SIC breakdown in type

**Business and insurance**
- W-9 on file (date received)
- Pilot's own non-owned aircraft liability certificate of insurance — carrier, limits, policy number, **expiration date**
- Stated day rate by category, with the date and the medium it was confirmed in (see Section 6)
- Positioning, hold day, and cancellation terms as the pilot states them
- Invoicing details and payment terms the pilot expects

**Operational**
- Categories the pilot is offered on: piston single / piston twin / turboprop / light jet / mentor-SIC
- Geography and radius he will actually take
- Typical response time; whether he'll take same-day
- Notes on prior underwriter outcomes — approved, declined, or endorsement required, and for which owner's policy. Record the outcome factually. Do not record opinions about the pilot's ability.

**History**
- Every job offered, accepted, declined, completed. Date, aircraft, owner, outcome
- Any reliability event: late, no-show, cancelled inside 24 hours, professionalism issue
- Whether the owner would use him again

### 4.2 Refresh cadence

- **Quarterly sweep** — one email to the whole bench asking for updated times, any new type ratings or recurrent completions, and any change to rate or availability. Cheap, and it also keeps AMG in front of the bench between jobs.
- **On every offer** — confirm times as of today and confirm the pilot's rate has not changed before it is quoted anywhere.
- **On every completed job** — update times, log the outcome, note underwriter result.
- **Thirty days before any expiring item** — direct contact, not a mass email.

### 4.3 Items that expire and must be re-checked before every launch

Treat all of these as stale until re-confirmed at the time of the job:

| Item | Why it expires | Re-check |
|---|---|---|
| Medical certificate | Class and age dependent | Every offer |
| Recurrent training completion | Annual or 12/24-month cycle, and underwriter-driven | Every offer |
| 61.55(b) SIC currency | Preceding 12 calendar months, per make and model | Every SIC offer |
| 61.57 landing currency (day/night) | 90 days | Every offer |
| Flight review / 61.56 | 24 calendar months | Every offer |
| Instrument currency / IPC | 6 calendar months of experience, then IPC | Every IFR offer |
| Pilot's non-owned liability COI | Annual policy term | Every offer |
| Recent and make-and-model times | Change constantly; drive underwriter decisions | Every offer |
| Broker/underwriter approval naming the pilot | Policy- and trip-specific; never transfers between owners or trips | **Every single flight — no exception** |

The last line is the one that matters. An approval email for Owner A's Baron says nothing about Owner B's King Air, and an approval from three months ago says nothing about today. For turbines, assume no usable open pilot warranty, assume a written endorsement is required, and assume 24–72 hours. Tell the pilot that up front so a 48-hour wait reads as process rather than as AMG stalling.

## 5. Bench targets

Sized for the first 10 jobs, realistically weighted: most early work will be piston and light-twin ferry out of independent shops, with a smaller number of turboprop and light jet coverage jobs that carry the real revenue and the longer approval clock.

**The rule of three.** For any category AMG will discuss with an owner, three confirmed pilots must be on the bench. Not one. One is a coin flip on the day of a callout; three survives one being on a trip and one being out of currency. AMG does not discuss a category it cannot staff three deep.

### 5.1 Targets by category

| Category | Target on bench | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Piston single/twin ferry (South FL, will travel in-state) | 6 | Highest job volume, lowest rate, highest churn — instructors take airline classes and disappear |
| Turboprop ferry / reposition (King Air, PC-12, TBM class) | 4 | Longer approval lead time; make-and-model time is the binding constraint |
| Light jet ferry / reposition | 3 | Small population; each one takes real work to source |
| Turboprop trip coverage (full trip, owner's mission) | 3 | Different animal from ferry — passenger-facing, higher standard |
| Light jet trip coverage | 3 | Same, and the type rating narrows it further |
| Mentor / insurance-required second pilot | 3–4 | Type-club sourced; **the private-pilot failure mode lives here** |
| **Distinct individuals** | **18–22** | Many pilots cover two or three categories; overlap is expected and fine |

### 5.2 Timeline

| By | Milestone |
|---|---|
| **Day 30** | 8 pilots contacted and in conversation. 4 complete bench records with documents on file. At least 2 piston-capable pilots who can be at an airplane in South Florida within 48 hours. 5 written rate confirmations collected. |
| **Day 60** | 12 complete records. Three-deep in piston ferry. Two turboprop pilots with documented make-and-model time. **Twelve written rate confirmations complete** — the published day-rate bands unblock here. |
| **Day 90** | 18 complete records. Three-deep in piston ferry, turboprop ferry, and mentor/SIC. At least one light jet pilot who has actually been through an underwriter approval with an AMG owner. |
| **Day 120** | Full table above. Quarterly refresh cycle running on schedule. |

### 5.3 The geographic reality

South Florida — FXE, PMP, BCT, TMB, OPF, FLL, PBI — is dense enough that 18 to 22 pilots is achievable inside 120 days on manual outreach. It thins fast north and west. Treat Tampa, Orlando, Jacksonville, Naples and Stuart as separate sourcing efforts with their own local names, and be honest with an owner when a job is outside the area where AMG has real depth. Two pilots on the Gulf coast is not a Gulf coast bench.

### 5.4 Do not sell ahead of the bench

If a category is not three deep, AMG does not put it in a proposal, name it on a call, print it on the one-pager, or quote it in an email. **This rule binds documents 03 and 05 as well as this one.** Before any price sheet or email sequence goes out, check the Tab 2 bench counts by category and delete every category that is not three deep. The correct sentence to an owner is: "That's outside what I can staff right now. I'd rather tell you that than take the job and miss." That sentence costs one job and preserves the referral source.

## 6. The rate conversation

Two goals, in this order: find out what the pilot actually charges without anchoring him, and build the twelve written confirmations the published day-rate bands are blocked on.

### 6.1 Ask open, and shut up

The question, verbatim:

> "What's your day rate for a [piston twin ferry / King Air trip], and how do you handle positioning and hold days?"

Then stop talking. The silence is the tool. The first number out of his mouth is the real number; anything after AMG names a figure is a negotiation against AMG's anchor and is worthless as market data.

**Do not** say "the range I'm seeing is…", "most guys are around…", or "I've got a budget of…". Not on the first conversation. Those sentences destroy the data point and cost money forever, because the bands published later get built on them.

If he asks first — "what does it pay?" — the answer is:

> "It's the owner's money, not mine, so the owner pays your rate directly and I don't mark it up. I'd rather quote your number than tell you mine. What do you charge?"

If he pushes a second time, give a genuine range and mark the data point as **anchored** in the record so it does not count toward the twelve. Do not lie about having no idea.

### 6.2 What to capture, every time

Rate alone is not a number you can quote from. Capture:

1. Day rate, by category (ferry vs full trip differ — often materially)
2. What a "day" means to him: duty day, calendar day, travel day
3. Positioning — does he charge for travel to the airplane, at what rate, and who buys the airline ticket
4. Hold / weather / AOG days — full rate, half rate, or nothing
5. Cancellation — how much notice before it becomes billable, and at what percentage
6. Hotel, rental car, meals — expected or absorbed
7. Minimum — some pilots have a two-day minimum on anything requiring travel
8. Whether the number changes for repeat work

Positioning note: if AMG ever fronts an airline positioning ticket, it is reimbursed by the owner as a documented AMG out-of-pocket **at cost** — never as an hourly or per-leg amount, and never as pilot pay. Say this to the pilot so nobody expects AMG to be paying him through a ticket.

### 6.3 Building the twelve

The set the bands need:

| Segment | Written confirmations needed |
|---|---|
| Piston ferry | 4 |
| Turboprop ferry / reposition | 3 |
| Light jet ferry / reposition | 2 |
| Turboprop or light jet full trip | 3 |
| **Total** | **12** |

**"Written" means the pilot's own words in text he sent** — email or a message he typed. Not AMG's transcription of a phone call. The conversation happens on the phone; the confirmation happens after, and AMG asks for it:

> "Can you send me that in an email so I've got your rate on file correctly? Just your day rate for [category], how you handle positioning and hold days, and your minimum if you have one."

Pilots send it. It is in their interest to have their number on file.

Rules for the set:
- One pilot, one data point per category. Twelve confirmations from four pilots is not twelve data points.
- Exclude anchored responses. Mark and keep them, but do not count them.
- Record the date. A rate from nine months ago is a stale rate.
- Two-party consent applies in Florida (Fla. Stat. §934.03). Do not record a rate call without the pilot's consent on the recording. Take notes instead — it is faster anyway.

### 6.4 What the collected data is for, and what it is not for

It sets the published pilot day-rate bands AMG shows an owner, and it sizes the coordination fee against pilot spend (the soft cap is 25–30% of pilot spend; the flat fee breaks above two days and day-scaling is the recommended fix — see the pricing work).

It is **not** a tool for pushing a pilot's rate down. AMG takes no markup and has no financial interest in the pilot's number. Telling a pilot "another guy quoted me less" gets AMG a reputation in a market of a few hundred people and saves AMG nothing, because AMG is not the one paying. The owner pays the pilot's rate. AMG's job is to quote it accurately.

## 7. What disqualifies a pilot

### 7.1 Hard disqualifiers — no discretion, no exception

1. **Private pilot certificate only.** 14 CFR 61.113(a) bars a private pilot from acting as PIC of an aircraft carrying passengers or property for compensation or hire, or from acting as PIC for compensation or hire. Commercial or ATP, always. **This breaks most often on mentor work** — an owner says "he's just riding along," or the pilot himself says he's only there for insurance. If he is being paid to be in the airplane, he needs a commercial certificate. If he is not being paid, AMG has no business coordinating it. There is no version of this AMG works around.
2. **No current medical**, or a medical that does not cover the operation.
3. **Not current** — 61.56 flight review, 61.57 landings, or for SIC use, 61.55(b) within the preceding 12 calendar months in that make and model.
4. **Missing required type rating** for the aircraft.
5. **Won't produce documents.** Certificates, medical, recurrent completion, logbook times, W-9, non-owned COI. A pilot who won't send documents to AMG will not survive an underwriter either.
6. **No non-owned aircraft liability coverage of his own**, and unwilling to get it.
7. **Declined by the owner's broker or underwriter for this trip.** Not a judgment on the pilot — the answer is still no for that flight.
8. **Won't work under a written engagement.**
9. **Misrepresents times, currency, or certificates.** Any discrepancy that isn't an honest arithmetic error ends the relationship permanently. AMG is putting these documents in front of an underwriter; a fabrication becomes AMG's material misrepresentation problem, and Fla. Stat. §627.409 lets an insurer rescind over exactly that.
10. **Wants to negotiate around the insurance gate** — "I've flown it before," "the owner said it's fine," "we can sort the endorsement after." That instinct will eventually cost somebody a hull.

### 7.2 Soft disqualifiers — track, do not necessarily use

Judgment calls. Record the fact, decide per job:

- Time in type below what the underwriter will accept for this specific policy. Not a character flaw; just a no for that airplane. Often a yes for a different one.
- Total time below the open pilot warranty floor — common with time-building instructors.
- No-show, late arrival, or a cancellation inside 24 hours. Once is a data point. Twice ends it.
- Talks about an owner's business or an owner's airplane in public or in a group. Immediate stop; this market runs on discretion.
- Tries to go around AMG to the owner after being introduced. Ends it, quietly and permanently.
- Vague or shifting answers about certificates, times, or rate.
- Pressure to fly when the answer should be no. The go/no-go is the PIC's and AMG never influences it — but a pilot who is visibly bad at that decision is a pilot AMG should not be putting in front of owners.

### 7.3 Declining gracefully

South Florida aviation is a small market with long memories. Every pilot AMG turns down is a potential referral source for two others, and a potential customer's chief pilot in three years. Decline cleanly.

**Principles.** Be quick — a slow no is worse than a fast one. Give the real reason if it is a fact (certificate, currency, time in type, underwriter). Do not give a reason if it is a judgment call — "not a fit for this one" is complete and honest enough. Never say "we didn't think you were good enough." Never blame the owner or the underwriter for a decision that was AMG's. And never leave the door falsely open: do not say "I'll keep you in mind" if the answer is permanent.

**Private pilot — the most common one, and the one most likely to offend.**

> "I can't use you on this and I want to be straight about why: you'd be getting paid to be PIC, and 61.113(a) doesn't allow that on a private certificate. It's not about your flying. If you get the commercial, call me — the mentor and second-pilot work is where I have the most demand and the least supply."

That is true, it is specific, and it hands him something to do about it.

**Time in type / underwriter declined.**

> "The owner's underwriter came back needing more make-and-model time than you have in this one. That's their number, not mine, and it's specific to this policy. If you build time in type, tell me — you're on my list for the [other category] work where it isn't the constraint."

**Not a fit, judgment call.**

> "I'm going a different direction on this one. I appreciate you getting back to me quickly."

Nothing more. Do not explain a judgment call; explanation invites argument and produces a grudge.

**Reliability — the second strike.**

> "I'm going to leave it here. Two of these haven't gone the way we planned and I've got an owner on the other end of them. No hard feelings on my side."

Say it once, in private, and never repeat it to anyone else in the market. AMG does not blacklist people out loud. Reputation damage travels back.

**Nobody hears about it.** Whatever the reason a pilot is off the bench, it stays between AMG and that pilot. Discussing a pilot's declination with a shop, an owner, or another pilot is how a small market decides AMG is not safe to deal with.

## 8. Weekly bench discipline

Recruiting fails when it becomes something to do after the selling is done. Fixed time, every week, regardless of demand-side load:

- **Monday, 30 min** — 3 new pilot contacts initiated (call, DM, or classified reply). Log every one.
- **Wednesday, 30 min** — follow up on everyone contacted last week. Most replies come on the second touch.
- **Friday, 20 min** — update bench records from the week: new documents, new times, completed jobs, expiring items in the next 30 days.
- **Monthly** — count the bench against the Section 5.1 table by category. Any category not three deep is the next month's recruiting priority, ahead of everything else.
- **Quarterly** — the refresh sweep in Section 4.2.

A category that has been under three deep for two consecutive months comes off the list of things AMG offers until it is fixed.

## 9. Open items

These are decisions or confirmations Tony owns; nothing below should be resolved by guessing.

1. **Hold-day and cancellation policy** — AMG needs a standard position to state to pilots before it is negotiated per job. AMG does not pay it (the owner does), but AMG needs a default to put in the owner's agreement and quote to the pilot consistently.
2. **What AMG signs with a pilot, if anything.** The owner engages the pilot; AMG coordinates. Whether AMG holds a non-solicitation or a simple bench participation acknowledgment with each pilot — and whether that risks looking like AMG is furnishing crew — needs a legal read before any pilot is asked to sign anything.
3. **Referral fees to pilots who refer other pilots.** Whether AMG pays anything, and if so how, against the commercial-bribery and employer-consent line in the operating facts.
4. **Whether AMG's own insurance application language covers a bench** — the four services must be described verbatim on the application (Fla. Stat. §627.409). Confirm the carrier understands that AMG maintains a roster of independent contract pilots but does not employ or supply them as a package with an aircraft.
5. **Trigger point for the second market** (Tampa / Orlando). At what job volume does AMG start recruiting outside South Florida rather than declining the work.
